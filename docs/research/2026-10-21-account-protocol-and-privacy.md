# Unify the Account First, Encrypt It Later

The protocol design that makes all five of Mercury's planned products easy is a single Mercury program that owns a per-user account on Solana, a program-derived address (PDA) holding the user's USDC, xStocks, earn receipts and one debt ledger, with earn, stock-backed credit, sends, card settlement and baskets written as instructions of that program and every instruction ending in the same health check. That is Gearbox's credit account fused with the single-ledger cross-margin Drift and Mango run on Solana, and it fits Solana's limits better than a smart account calling several protocols, because nested calls are still capped at four levels, the new 4,096-byte transactions carry no lookup tables and only 64 accounts, and health cannot be checked atomically once positions live in other programs. The card is what forces this design: once users spend against stocks, borrowed dollars and authorised-but-uncaptured card spend must stay under program control, which a wallet-held model cannot enforce. Bridge, the planned issuer, pulls USDC just in time through an SPL delegate on one token account per card, so Mercury has to run its own cash-earn-credit waterfall into a ring-fenced card buffer, record authorisation holds as liabilities, and absorb merchant captures that can arrive 31 days later; credit-model issuers such as Rain and Reap suit a stock-backed card better, but Rain's own Solana collateral contract lost about **$1.1M** in August 2026. The privacy that is live today hides amounts (Token-2022 confidential transfers, on mints Mercury controls) and links (screened, capped pools such as Umbra, or pooled settlement); no production lending market on any chain keeps collateral or debt private. Because Mercury is the only lender and runs KYC, it does not need trustless private lending: a target of _private from the public, visible to Mercury and its auditors_ turns private credit into a key-custody problem that Solana's existing proof tooling can partly address now. The largest risks sit in the permission and admin layers rather than the card rails, namely a new instruction that skips the health check, forged signatures in delegate modules (Gnosis Pay and Rain, both in 2026) and admin keys without timelocks (Drift, about **$285M**). The choices to settle early are the ones that cannot be undone: the account container, how much of the custody core stays upgradeable, the card issuer model, and the Token-2022 configuration of any token Mercury issues.

## One program-owned account with one health check fits Solana

### Every account design pairs a container, a gate and a final check

Gearbox v3 is the closest template. Its credit account is a smart-contract wallet that draws debt from Gearbox pools and must stay solvent once it has, and the borrowed funds never reach the user: they sit in the account, and the owner can use them only through the Credit Facade, which confines the account to allowlisted adapters for outside protocols ([Gearbox](https://dev.gearbox.finance/core/accounts)). Each owner multicall executes atomically and is **followed by one collateral check** ([Gearbox](https://dev.gearbox.finance/core/multicall)). The check path carries rules worth copying: withdrawals switch to the lower of two price feeds, new debt is refused while forbidden tokens are enabled or when debt already changed in the same block, and a full repayment skips the check entirely ([ICreditFacadeV3Multicall](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Gearbox-protocol/core-v3/main/contracts/interfaces/ICreditFacadeV3Multicall.sol)). A lazy mode values hinted tokens first and stops once weighted collateral covers debt, which saves oracle reads ([Gearbox](https://dev.gearbox.finance/credit/multicall/full-check-params)). Bots act under a per-account permission bitmask, and Gearbox is candid that nothing beyond the collateral check runs after a bot's multicall, so slippage, minimum-health and destination limits must live in the bot itself ([Gearbox](https://dev.gearbox.finance/core/bots)). Liquidation takes over the whole account: anyone may act below health factor 1, the account must afterwards have repaid the pool, left the owner a minimum remainder and not increased its enabled collateral, and any loss halts borrowing in that credit manager until governance re-enables it ([Gearbox](https://dev.gearbox.finance/core/liquidation)). Because borrowed USDC never leaves the account, the protocol can count it as collateral, let it flow only to allowlisted destinations such as a card-settlement address, an earn vault or a swap, and liquidate it together with the stocks. Kamino and marginfi, by contrast, send borrowed tokens to the user's wallet and lose sight of them.

Euler v2's Ethereum Vault Connector (EVC) separates three questions: who may act, which vault controls the debt, and when solvency is checked. Each address gets 256 virtual sub-accounts, and owners grant operators a bitfield of the sub-accounts they may control ([Euler](https://docs.euler.finance/build/evc)). An account may enable up to 10 collaterals but only **one controller vault**, and only that controller can release itself, so a lender gives up its claim only once the debt is gone ([IEVC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/euler-xyz/ethereum-vault-connector/master/src/interfaces/IEthereumVaultConnector.sol)). Status checks are deferred to the end of the outermost batch; the liability vault seizes collateral through `controlCollateral`, which the collateral vault sees as an ordinary transfer; and an owner-only lockdown mode blocks external calls and leaves operators able only to remove themselves while still letting controllers liquidate ([EVC whitepaper](https://github.com/euler-xyz/ethereum-vault-connector/blob/master/docs/whitepaper.md)). Euler's docs also carry the single most important warning in this survey: a vault that fails to request the right check gets no protection from deferral ([Euler](https://docs.euler.finance/build/evc)). That is the general form of the bug behind Euler's 2023 loss and marginfi's 2025 near-miss.

Aave v4, live on Ethereum since 30 March 2026 with 3 Hubs and 10 Spokes ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-aave-v4-activation-on-ethereum-mainnet/24293)), scopes each user position to a Spoke and adds a risk premium to the borrow rate based on collateral mix ([Aave v4](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v4)). Its permission model is the cleanest found. Governance must register each position manager and the user must enable it; a Giver manager that only supplies or repays with its own funds needs no allowance, while a Taker manager that withdraws or borrows on the user's behalf needs per-reserve allowances ([Aave v4 Position Managers](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v4/positions/managers)). Hyperliquid's portfolio margin nets most aggressively: spot and perpetual positions share one balance, shortfalls are auto-borrowed against eligible collateral, and the safeguards are per-user borrow caps, a liquidation threshold set halfway between LTV and 1, and a backstop liquidator that takes over in 20% steps ([Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/portfolio-margin)). That auto-borrow, where spending beyond cash quietly draws credit, is exactly the experience Mercury wants for its card, though portfolio margin launched as a testnet pre-alpha with a $1k borrow cap ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/hyperliquid-launches-portfolio-margin-and-blp-pre-alpha)) and its mainnet status is unconfirmed. dYdX takes the opposite approach: sub-accounts 0–127 are cross-margined, while child sub-accounts up to 128,000 each hold one isolated position ([dYdX](https://docs.dydx.exchange/api_integration-trading/isolated_markets)).

Solana's own lenders show what the runtime tolerates. A Kamino obligation holds at most **8 deposits and 5 borrows** and must be refreshed with reserve prices before every operation ([klend architecture](https://mintlify.wiki/kamino-finance/klend/concepts/architecture)). A Drift sub-account holds 8 spot and 8 perpetual positions plus a delegate that, according to third-party indexer documentation, can do everything except withdraw ([drift-rs](https://mintlify.wiki/drift-labs/drift-rs/api/accounts/user); [Shyft](https://docs.shyft.to/solana-indexers/case-studies/drift/get-user-account-for-delegate)). Mango v4's health regions are the Solana-native deferred check: `HealthRegionBegin` reads the instructions sysvar, refuses to run under CPI, requires a matching end instruction, and allows only whitelisted order instructions in between, excluding deposits and withdrawals ([mango-v4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blockworks-foundation/mango-v4/dev/programs/mango-v4/src/instructions/health_region.rs)). marginfi's flash-loan flag works the opposite way, skipping health checks entirely while set ([Asymmetric Research](https://asymmetric.com/threat-contained-marginfi-flash-loan-vulnerability/)), and that difference becomes decisive in the security discussion below. Mango also carries a regulatory lesson for cross-margin accounts: it began winding down in January 2025 after an SEC settlement ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/334172/mango-markets-to-wind-down-in-wake-of-sec-settlement-dao-battle)).

Self-custodial card products have converged on the same four parts: a user-owned smart account, a narrow spend permission held by the card operator, a delay on every other outflow, and an optional debt module that lends against collateral in the same account. ether.fi Cash runs per-user Safes, a Cash Module, a Debt Manager and a permissionless Settlement Dispatcher, audited by Certora and Nethermind ([ether.fi cash-v3](https://github.com/etherfi-protocol/cash-v3)), and has processed more than **$800M of card spend** ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/ether-fi-cash-exceeds-800m-spend/)). Gnosis Pay gives each user a Safe whose Roles module fixes the spendable token, a daily limit and the recipient, an issuer-owned settlement Safe ([Gnosis Pay help](https://help.gnosispay.com/en/articles/8464758-how-is-the-gnosis-card-self-custodial)). Coinbase's Spend Permission Manager reduces the idea to a struct of spender, token, allowance and period ([Coinbase](https://github.com/coinbase/spend-permissions)). Rain keeps customer stablecoins in a contract the customer owns and extends a credit line against them ([Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/how-cards-became-the-trojan-horse-for-stablecoin-adoption)).

| Design                        | Account unit                                                         | Who may act                                  | When solvency is checked                          | Liquidation                               | What Mercury should take                             |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Gearbox v3                    | Isolated credit account per position                                 | Owner via the Credit Facade; bots by bitmask | After every multicall                             | Whole account, owner remainder guaranteed | Borrowed funds stay inside; allowlisted destinations |
| Euler EVC                     | 256 sub-accounts per address                                         | Owner, operators by bitfield, signed permits | Deferred to end of outermost batch                | Controller seizes via `controlCollateral` | One debt controller per sub-account; user lockdown   |
| Aave v4                       | Position per Spoke                                                   | User; registered Giver and Taker managers    | Per Spoke                                         | Repay only to a target health factor      | Giver/Taker split of delegate rights                 |
| Hyperliquid portfolio margin  | One unified balance                                                  | Owner                                        | Continuous margin ratio                           | Backstop takes over in 20% steps          | Auto-borrow on shortfall, with caps                  |
| Kamino, Drift, Mango (Solana) | One account with fixed position arrays (8/5, 8/8, sized at creation) | Owner; Drift delegate without withdrawals    | Refresh plus check per instruction; Mango regions | Partial, permissionless                   | Bounded arrays; whitelist inside deferred regions    |
| ether.fi, Gnosis Pay, Rain    | Per-user Safe or collateral contract                                 | Owner; card role with caps                   | At spend or settlement                            | Debt manager or issuer                    | Scoped card role plus delays on other outflows       |

### Solana's limits favour one program over a smart account that calls many

Four runtime facts in October 2026 decide the container. Nested CPI is still capped at four levels; SIMD-0268 raises it to eight, but mainnet activation is set for some time after Agave 4.4 with no date ([Solana upgrades](https://solana.com/upgrades/cpi-nesting-depth)). The v1 transaction format went live on 15 September 2026 and allows 4,096 bytes instead of 1,232, but supports no address lookup tables and lists at most **64 accounts** inline ([Solana upgrades](https://solana.com/upgrades/larger-transaction-sizes)). Blocks rose to 100M compute units on 29 July 2026, while any single writable account stays capped at **12M CU per block** ([Solana upgrades](https://solana.com/upgrades/100m-cu-blocks)). Rent has fallen to 5,080 lamports per byte with three further cuts scheduled, so a 1,000-byte account costs about 0.0057 SOL that the relayer can pay and reclaim on close ([Chainstack](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-rent-reduction-reclaim-excess-rent)).

| Container                                                    | Where assets sit                                          | How health is checked                                                 | Strengths                                                                                            | Costs                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Program-owned PDA                                         | Token accounts whose authority is the Mercury Account PDA | One function at the end of every instruction, or a Mango-style region | Atomic checks; borrowed funds stay inside; whole-account liquidation; one CPI hop to external venues | Whoever can upgrade the program governs every user's assets; Mercury must wrap each external venue        |
| B. Smart account (Swig, Squads) calling modules              | Smart-account PDA                                         | Across programs; needs a registry that rebuilds the EVC               | Mature signer and policy layer (passkeys, session keys, spend limits)                                | CPI depth and account count; no single program sees every position                                        |
| C. User wallet; protocols track positions (Kamino, marginfi) | Privy wallet                                              | Per protocol                                                          | Simplest for earn and single-protocol borrowing                                                      | Cannot enforce spend-against-collateral; one SPL delegate per token account; borrowed funds leave control |

Option B fails mainly on depth, since a chain such as smart account → Mercury → Jupiter Lend or Kamino → token program reaches the four-level cap ([Solana upgrades](https://solana.com/upgrades/cpi-nesting-depth)). Swig and Squads are best used in front of Option A as the user's signer and policy layer. Swig pairs an authority (Ed25519, secp256k1, passkey or another program, each with a session variant) with token limits, destination-scoped limits and program scopes, and was audited by Accretion in 2025 and Halborn in August 2026 ([Swig](https://github.com/anagrambuild/swig-wallet)); the Squads Smart Account Program is audited and formally verified by OtterSec and Certora, and its README warns that a compromised settings authority can rewrite thresholds and members ([Squads](https://github.com/Squads-Protocol/smart-account-program)). Option C fails on delegation: an SPL token account holds exactly one delegate with one approved amount, and a new approval replaces the old ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/approve-delegate)), so a card issuer and an auto-earn keeper cannot both hold rights over the same USDC account. Privy's policy engine can restrict program IDs and SPL transfer destinations, but it evaluates those rules off-chain in Privy's enclave at signing time ([Privy](https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/overview)), which makes it a useful extra layer rather than a protocol guarantee.

This refines the usual advice for a cash-yield product, which is to let users hold vault share tokens in their own Privy wallets. For earn alone that advice holds: a single Solana transaction can carry a lending withdrawal and a spend under one signature, with no container at all. Borrowing against stocks and card spend change the calculation, because both need funds the user cannot move between a check and a settlement. A Gearbox- and dYdX-style middle path keeps idle cash and earn shares in the wallet and opens a Mercury credit account PDA only when the user borrows or turns on the card, so the custody core holds only funds that need program enforcement and its blast radius is limited to borrowers and cardholders. The cost is money in two places, which the app has to present as one balance.

The transaction budget is tighter than it looks. Each xStock in a health check needs its token account, its mint (the dividend multiplier lives in the mint's Scaled UI Amount extension, and collateral value is raw amount times multiplier times price ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/scaled-ui-amount))) and its oracle, so eight stocks consume 24 of a v1 transaction's 64 accounts before any earn reserves, Jupiter route accounts or fee accounts appear. Two choices recover most of that budget. Tracking collateral balances in the PDA's own ledger, rather than reading token-account balances, both removes those accounts from the check and closes the donation attack that cost Cream about $130M, where tokens sent directly to a vault inflated the value it used as collateral ([Immunefi](https://immunefi.com/blog/bug-fix-reviews/hack-analysis-cream-finance-oct-2021/)). A compact on-chain price cache that a pusher updates with price and multiplier for every listed asset replaces sixteen accounts with one. A reasonable starting shape, following Kamino and Drift, is about eight stock slots, one or two cash and earn slots and one debt slot, and Mango shows slot counts can be creation parameters with on-chain resize logic ([mango-v4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blockworks-foundation/mango-v4/dev/programs/mango-v4/src/instructions/account_create.rs)). These are design inferences; no source measured the compute cost of an eight-position Token-2022 health check. Parallelism favours the same shape, since per-user accounts do not contend with each other, while one shared card-settlement vault written on every swipe would hit the 12M-CU-per-account ceiling and should be sharded or written in batches.

Option A has one real cost, and it is legal as much as technical. The PDA's authority is the Mercury program, so whoever can upgrade that program can rewrite the rules over every user's assets. US money-transmission analysis turns on whether a party has total independent control over the funds, and a co-signer that cannot unilaterally move funds falls outside it ([Covington on FinCEN 2019](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2019/10/fincen_issues_guidance_to_synthesize_regulatory_framework_for_virtual_currency.pdf)). An instantly upgradeable custody program undermines the self-custody claim that Privy keys otherwise support. The structure that protects the claim is a small custody core covering deposits, withdrawals, the health function, the permission table and liquidation, made either immutable or upgradeable only after a delay long enough for users to exit, with product modules upgraded separately so that no module change can move funds without passing the core's checks.

### Each product becomes an instruction against the same ledger

| Product               | What lives in the Mercury Account                                                                  | Instruction pattern                                 | Effect on health                                        | Who may trigger it                                                                          |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Earn                  | USDC; Jupiter Lend or Kamino receipts, or Mercury vault shares, valued with a haircut              | Deposit and withdraw by CPI, prepended to any spend | Adds collateral; withdrawals checked                    | User; auto-earn keeper with deposit-only (Giver) rights                                     |
| Borrow against stocks | xStock balances tracked internally; one debt record as single controller                           | Borrow, repay, add or remove collateral             | Borrow and withdraw checked; repay always allowed       | User; anyone can repay; liquidation by Mercury or permissionless keepers                    |
| Spend and sends       | USDC with optional auto-borrow                                                                     | Send or swap through allowlisted routes             | Checked at the end                                      | User only; agents with capped, destination-pinned rights                                    |
| Card                  | Ring-fenced card-buffer token account carrying the issuer's delegate; pending holds as a liability | Top up through the waterfall; record hold; settle   | Top-ups checked; holds reduce spending power            | Card-authorisation service (holds); capped top-up keeper; issuer pulls from the buffer only |
| Baskets               | Constituent xStock positions plus target weights                                                   | Rebalance through an allowlisted swap adapter       | Each constituent uses a collateral slot and price input | User; rebalancing keeper confined to basket mints with its own slippage bounds              |

Earn keeps its familiar mechanics, but once the PDA holds the receipts, Mercury's program becomes a direct counterparty of the external venues. Kamino changed its contracts in December 2025 to block Jupiter's program after Jupiter Lend shipped a one-click migration tool ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/jup-lend-vs-kamino)), and Kamino's flash loans already reject CPI ([klend](https://mintlify.wiki/kamino-finance/klend/advanced/flash-loans)), so any venue can equally refuse calls from Mercury; that argues for capped external allocations behind a Mercury-controlled vault path. Credit follows the EVC's one-controller rule: a credit line, a margin product and a leveraged basket must share one debt ledger inside one program, or live in separate sub-accounts, so that two lenders never compete for the same collateral. The collateral itself carries issuer powers. Backed's xStocks are Token-2022 mints with a **permanent delegate**, a **pausable** switch and a Scaled UI multiplier ([Solscan: TSLAx](https://solscan.io/token/XsDoVfqeBukxuZHWhdvWHBhgEHjGNst4MLodqsJHzoB)), so Backed could burn or freeze collateral inside a Mercury Account while the debt remains, and a pause would block liquidation transfers; Ondo's Solana stock tokens carry no permanent delegate ([Solscan: TSLAon](https://solscan.io/token/KeGv7bsfR4MheC1CkmnAVceoApjrkvBhHYjWb67ondo)). A collateral registry should record each mint's extensions and authorities at listing and fail closed if any change. Baskets are the product most constrained by the account budget, because each constituent costs a collateral slot and a price input, so a broad basket needs the price cache, counts only its largest constituents toward borrowing power, or becomes a single basket token with its own valuation and one more oracle to defend. A rebalancing keeper inherits Gearbox's bot lesson that the core check enforces solvency only, so trade limits belong in the keeper's own permission. These basket points are reasoning from the account sources; the research did not cover a live tokenized-stock basket protocol on Solana.

## Card programs settle against the account through a debit authority, a hold and a recovery path

### Bridge fits the stack but pulls from one token account per card

Bridge offers three funding strategies: non-custodial, where it pulls from a customer-controlled wallet at each authorisation; a custodial Bridge Wallet debited at each authorisation; and a Stripe Financial Account. In each, **a wallet can be linked to only one card at a time** ([Bridge docs](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/funding-strategies)). On Solana, card provisioning registers a program delegate, after which the app adds an SPL approve to a Bridge PDA derived from the program's merchant ID, the mint and the token account, with an optional sponsored fee payer; the pull is submitted at authorisation but completes asynchronously, a follow-up webhook reports the chain, amount and transaction hash, and incremental authorisations for hotels or fuel submit additional pulls ([Bridge docs](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/noncustodial.md)). Bridge declines when the approval is inactive or too small or the wallet lacks funds, and Privy's Bridge recipe warns that networks can pull funds without the backend's involvement, suggesting either an off-chain Privy policy that blocks other outbound transfers or webhook-driven on-chain bookkeeping ([Privy recipe](https://docs.privy.io/recipes/bridge-cards)).

Bridge's reach is the weak point for a non-US base. Visa and Bridge said the product was live in 18 countries on 3 March 2026 and targeted more than 100 by year-end ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22206.html)); Bridge said 30 countries in April 2026, with Lead Bank, a US bank, as the only disclosed issuer ([Bridge blog](https://www.bridge.xyz/blog/stablecoin-backed-cards-are-now-integrated-with-stripe-issuing)); and its product page lists Africa, Latin America and the US as live with **the EU and APAC still planned for 2026**. Programs keep 100% of interchange, and cardholders spending in their KYC country on a localized BIN avoid a roughly 1% cross-border fee ([Bridge](https://www.bridge.xyz/product/cards)). Bridge holds a MiCA CASP authorisation and an EMI licence in Luxembourg ([The Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/07/27/stripe-bridge-stablecoin-global/)), runs its own KYC, and revokes an endorsement if KYC is not completed within 24 hours ([Privy recipe](https://docs.privy.io/recipes/bridge-cards)). Mercury needs written confirmation of the issuing entity and BIN for each target country.

Fitting Bridge into the Mercury Account takes one structural decision: the card should point at a dedicated **card-buffer token account** owned by the Mercury Account PDA, with the Mercury program signing the SPL approve to Bridge's PDA through `invoke_signed`. Bridge's documentation describes a customer-controlled wallet, not a program-owned token account, so whether Bridge accepts a PDA-owned source is the first question to put to it. Once approved, Bridge pulls through the token program directly and Mercury's health check never runs at pull time, so the buffer must be treated as already spent: its balance counts as committed card cash, not collateral. The Gearbox invariant moves to the top-up instruction, the only path into the buffer, which runs the full health check and executes the waterfall of cash first, then unwinding earn, then drawing credit. Two products resemble that cascade. Baanx charges delegated wallets in priority order until one succeeds ([Baanx](https://docs.baanx.com/guides/delegation/overview.md)), and ether.fi distinguishes Direct Pay, which moves tokens from the vault to the settlement dispatcher, from Credit mode, which borrows against vault collateral and can carry a delay when switched on ([ether.fi docs](https://etherfi.gitbook.io/etherfi/products/cash/cash-modules)). Mercury's automatic version needs a top-up keeper with a narrowly scoped role that keeps the buffer above expected authorisations, plus bookkeeping that keeps the delegated allowance in step with the buffer.

### Network timing, not Solana finality, sets the hold requirements

Solana confirms in about a second, but the card network keeps its own clock. On Stripe Issuing, which Bridge cards run on, captures normally land within 24 hours but hotels, airlines and car-rental firms can capture **up to 31 days after authorisation**; captures against approved authorisations always succeed regardless of card controls; restaurants and transport over-capture; force captures arrive after a decline or with no authorisation at all; and refunds can arrive unlinked to any purchase and are processed even on a cancelled card ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/purchases/transactions)). The issuer therefore always carries a gap between what was authorised and what is finally owed, and self-custodial programs close it by stopping users from emptying the account first. Gnosis Pay cuts the spendable balance the moment a withdrawal starts, runs a Delay module with a **25-hour delay** whose direct use blocks the card, and sets its on-chain Roles limit at $20K per token, twice the card limit, because clearing and settlement can take one to four days ([Gnosis Pay docs](https://docs.gnosispay.com/concepts/safe/overview.md)); settlement runs through Monavate in 12-hour batches ([Gnosis Pay architecture](https://docs.gnosispay.com/concepts/architecture.md)). Older help material described a three-minute delay, and whether the delay was lengthened after the June 2026 exploit was not established. ether.fi gates spending behind a dedicated wallet role, applies daily and monthly limit changes only after a delay, and fails withdrawals until their delay has passed ([ether.fi docs](https://etherfi.gitbook.io/etherfi/products/cash/cash-modules)).

| What the issuer needs               | Gnosis Pay                                                             | ether.fi Cash                              | Bridge (non-custodial)              | Mercury Account equivalent                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scoped debit authority              | Roles module: token, daily limit, fixed recipient                      | `spend` behind a wallet role               | SPL approve to a Bridge PDA         | Delegate on the card buffer plus a program-level card role                                |
| Funds held until capture            | Allowance at 2x the card limit; spendable cut when a withdrawal starts | Withdrawal delay                           | Not documented                      | Pending holds as a liability, capped at the card limit, expiring after the capture window |
| Limit changes not instant           | Delay module                                                           | Changes apply after a configured delay     | Not documented                      | Timelocked increases; instant decreases                                                   |
| Real-time spending power            | Balance and statement APIs                                             | `canSpend` over balance or borrowing power | Declines on low approval or balance | One read: buffer + cash + earn after haircut + available credit − holds                   |
| Recovery of force and over-captures | Not documented                                                         | Debt manager                               | Settled from program balance        | Receivable secured by a lien on earn and the credit line                                  |

The hold record is itself a power to bound, because a service that can write holds can freeze a user's funds. Holds should be capped at the card limit, expire after the capture window, and be writable only by a role that can add liabilities but never move value, closer to Aave's Giver than its Taker. A force capture with no matching authorisation is paid from the program's balance and leaves Mercury or Bridge holding a claim against the user, so the account also needs a written negative-balance policy.

### Credit-model issuers trade control for a better fit with stock-backed spend

The alternatives split into delegate-pull programs and credit programs, and credit fits stock-backed spend more naturally. Rain is a Visa principal member whose US cards are issued by Cross River Bank and which says it holds approvals in dozens of countries without naming them ([Rain](https://rain.xyz/resources/how-to-launch-global-card-programs-without-starting-over-in-every-market)); it underwrites credit against stablecoins in a contract, settles with Visa daily in USDC ([Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/how-cards-became-the-trojan-horse-for-stablecoin-adoption)), authorises against on-chain balances in under 200 ms ([Rain](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/giving-stablecoins-real-world-spending-power)), and exposes credit limit, spending power and balance due through an API while deploying and operating each user's collateral contract itself ([Crossmint](https://docs.crossmint.com/wallets/guides/credit-cards)). It grew about 38x in 2025 to more than $3B of annualised volume ([insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/the-state-of-stablecoin-cards)). Rain's Solana record also holds the most relevant warning in the card research. On 28 August 2026 an outdated version of its card-collateral contract verified two Ed25519 signature instructions per withdrawal, and an attacker pointed the second instruction's offsets at the first, so **one attacker-controlled signature counted as two**; the attacker then called admin and withdrawal paths thousands of times over about two and a half hours, Avici lost $500,859 across 1,685 users and Tria about $431,945, around $1.1M went to Tornado Cash, and Blockaid found the same bytecode in four deployments ([Blockaid](https://blockaid.io/blog/11m-rain-ecosystem-exploit-how-onchain-monitoring-gives-stablecoin-card-issuers-fleet-level-coverage)). Avici refunded users plus 10%, which shows who bears the loss when an outsourced contract fails.

Reap, a Visa principal member in Hong Kong and Mexico, announced stablecoin-collateralised credit card programs across more than 100 markets in September 2026 ([Reap](https://reap.global/newsroom/reap-visa-launch-100-markets-stablecoin-card-program)). Baanx, now owned by Exodus, supports SPL delegation on Solana under UK and EU EMI licences ([Baanx](https://docs.baanx.com/guides/delegation/overview.md); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1656001/exodus-movement-acquires-outstanding-shares-of-monavate-holdings-limited-and-baanx-com-ltd)). Gnosis Pay has wide regional coverage but runs only on Gnosis Chain and Celo ([Gnosis Pay](https://docs.gnosispay.com/)). Counterparty failure is real: Kulipa, a Paris issuer, shut down on 29 July 2026 and cut off more than 120,000 cards including Solflare's, though because those programs pulled at purchase, no customer balances sat at Kulipa ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/ready-shuts-down-card-program-after-kulipa-wind-down)).

A credit-model issuer removes real-time stock pricing from Mercury's authorisation path and earns credit rather than debit interchange. The catch is that Rain's model assumes Rain runs the collateral contract, which collides with Mercury owning the account, and the research found no evidence that Rain or Reap will underwrite against a third-party program and act only as a collector against it. Mercury's feature would be distinctive either way: no Solana card surveyed accepts tokenized stocks as card collateral, since KAST is custodial and Avici lends against deposits ([The Grid](https://thegrid.id/profiles/avici)). Kulipa's collapse argues for a second provider and portable KYC from the start.

### Interchange is thin, so the card earns through the account

EU rules cap consumer interchange at 0.2% for debit and 0.3% for credit ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/BGR/Publication/EU-cap-on-interchange-fees-for-card-based-payments2)), and non-EEA cards used in the EEA face the same caps in store and 1.15% and 1.5% online until November 2029 ([Fintech Futures](https://fintechfutures.com/?p=15357476)), so a 2% cashback card loses money there. The durable revenue sits in the account instead, in float and earn on the USDC buffer and in interest on stock-backed credit drawn by card spend. ether.fi credits its card with more than doubling deposits into its earn product in the card's first year ([Rain case study](https://www.rain.xyz/resources/from-onchain-assets-to-everyday-liquidity-how-ether-fi-uses-stablecoin-cards-to-drive-engagement-revenue-and-retention)). That matters because an in-house lending market can only pay depositors what borrowers pay into it, and Kamino's entire xStocks market carried only about $5.2M of USDC debt in October 2026 ([Kamino API](https://api.kamino.finance/kamino-market/5wJeMrUYECGq41fxRESKALVcHnNX26TAWy4W98yULsua/reserves/metrics?env=mainnet-beta)); a card that borrows against stocks is the product that creates borrowing demand, which makes it an earn product in disguise. Two EU rules apply, offered as general observations rather than legal advice. The recast Consumer Credit Directive applies from **20 November 2026** to credit up to €100,000 ([A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/files/uploads/news_insights_pub/CCD2_Report_F.pdf)), and its predecessor's investor-credit exclusion was tied to transactions in financial instruments ([Directive 2008/48/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2008/48/chapter/I)), so a stock-backed line that funds groceries likely falls inside it. The Transfer of Funds Regulation requires CASPs to verify ownership of a self-hosted address for transfers above €1,000 ([Notabene](https://notabene.id/post/a-deep-dive-into-self-hosted-wallet-transaction-requirements-under-the-eu-tfr)), which plausibly reaches Bridge's pulls from a PDA buffer.

## Live privacy hides amounts and links, while private credit remains research

### What each layer of the account can hide today

| Account layer             | Live on Solana mainnet                                                                            | Alpha, beta or devnet                                                                       | Research or pitch only                                            | Visible regardless                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Balances                  | Token-2022 confidential transfers on mints with the extension                                     | Helius confidential rings (devnet); Arcium C-SPL (early access, mainnet unconfirmed)        | —                                                                 | Token-account addresses, the mint, deposit and withdrawal amounts, timing                        |
| Earn                      | Confidential transfers on a Mercury-issued vault share mint, if set at creation                   | —                                                                                           | —                                                                 | Share price and vault totals                                                                     |
| Borrowing and liquidation | Nothing                                                                                           | Arcium MPC Mainnet Alpha (no lending app); PDA confidential-escrow prototype (October 2026) | Zama, Fhenix and Inco lending designs; zkredit and null402 claims | Liquidation events, auction lots, timing                                                         |
| Identity                  | Solana Attestation Service (public, no ZK)                                                        | —                                                                                           | ZK KYC on Solana (no primary-source deployments found)            | That a wallet holds Mercury's attestation                                                        |
| Payments and sends        | Confidential amounts; Umbra shielded pool (public since March 2026, capped); Cloak (private beta) | Helius anonymous rings; MagicBlock private rollups (sources conflict on status)             | —                                                                 | Entry to and exit from pools                                                                     |
| Card spend                | Nothing from issuer, processor or network                                                         | —                                                                                           | ZK-note cards such as Payy                                        | Merchant, category, amount and location at the issuer; on-chain pulls under just-in-time funding |

Balance privacy is live but narrow. Confidential transfers were disabled on 19 June 2025 after a proof-forgery flaw and re-enabled on 4 June 2026, and for that whole year confidential balances could not move at all ([xroot.dev](https://xroot.dev/blog/solana-confidential-transfers-kill-switch-proof-cost)). The feature hides amounts and balances but never the parties, and moving funds between public and confidential balances shows the amount ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer)). A mint must opt in at creation ([SPL docs](https://www.solana-program.com/docs/confidential-balances)), and USDC and USDT on Solana use the legacy SPL Token program with no extensions ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/solana-token-extensions-payments)), so the Mercury Account's cash cannot be confidential while it sits as USDC. It would have to sit as a Mercury-issued token, either a vault share or a branded dollar whose confidential-transfer authority Mercury has negotiated. PYUSD, USDG and xStocks have all initialised the extension with manual approval and no auditor key, and on-chain checks found essentially no configured confidential accounts, two of 495,503 in PYUSD's case ([Solscan: PYUSD](https://solscan.io/token/2b1kV6DkPAnxd5ixfnxCpjxmKwqjjaYmCZfHsFu24GXo)). The costs are moderate: range proofs cost 111k–368k compute units and each enabled account adds about 295 bytes, or 0.0015 SOL of rent ([xroot.dev](https://xroot.dev/blog/solana-confidential-transfers-kill-switch-proof-cost); [Solana integration guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/integration-guide)).

The capability that matters most for a program-owned account, whether a program can use confidential balances at all, shifted in October 2026. A Solana program cannot decrypt a confidential balance, but it can consume zero-knowledge statements about one: the ZK ElGamal Proof program exposes equality, range, zero-balance and validity proofs that any program can read from instruction data or from persistent context accounts, which the docs recommend when PDAs need proofs ([Anza](https://docs.anza.xyz/runtime/zk-elgamal-proof)). The Solana Foundation's delivery-versus-payment prototype, opened 7 October 2026, shows the working pattern. A PDA owns a Token-2022 confidential escrow and moves it only by CPI, escrow viewing keys are derived per swap under an operator master key that can sit in a hardware security module, ciphertext-equality proofs bind settlement to stored amounts, and the core transactions fit in 553 and 729 bytes ([solana-foundation/dvp #19](https://github.com/solana-foundation/dvp/issues/19)). The same prototype lists its hazards plainly: an incoming pending credit invalidates prepared proofs, losing every seed copy and the master key makes funds unrecoverable, no confidential exit works while the proof program is disabled, and any mint auditor sees every amount.

Private lending does not exist in production anywhere. Zama's FHE protocol has been live on Ethereum since 30 December 2025, but its live apps are transfers, staking and bridging, and it does not support Solana ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/zama-protocol-update-new-sdk-delegated-decryption-developer-tooling-and-protocol-apps)); its own lending design admits that encrypted values complicate liquidation triggers ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/onboard-the-next-trillions-in-defi-with-confidential-lending)). Fhenix's design keeps collateral and debt encrypted and publishes only a healthy-or-liquidatable bit, on testnets ([Fhenix](https://www.fhenix.io/blog/encrypted-lending-ethereum-fully-homomorphic-encryption-private-defi)). Inco's confidential tokens run on Solana devnet ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/lightning)). Arcium's MPC network reached Solana Mainnet Alpha on 2 February 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/387564/arcium-launches-privacy-preserving-mainnet-alpha-on-solana-as-umbra-debuts-shielded-finance-layer)), but its C-SPL standard, which would let PDAs hold and move private balances ([Arcium](https://www.arcium.com/build)), could not be confirmed on mainnet. Every published design converges on encrypted positions, a compute network that evaluates health against public oracle prices and reveals one bit, and disclosure only at liquidation, and none fully specifies who learns what at that moment.

### Mercury as sole lender turns private credit into key custody

Mercury's credit line is not open DeFi. Mercury is the only lender and a KYC'd intermediary, and as risk manager it can hold decryption rights to every position, which shrinks the goal from trustless private lending to _private from the public and other users, fully visible to Mercury risk and its auditors_. That version can be assembled from existing parts. Collateral denominated in Mercury-issued tokens can sit in a PDA-owned confidential escrow following the delivery-versus-payment pattern, with escrow keys derived under a Mercury HSM key; a Mercury keeper holding those keys produces the range proof that collateral times price minus debt is non-negative; and the program verifies that proof against the on-chain ciphertexts. ElGamal's additive homomorphism plus multiplication by a public price is in principle enough to form that expression, but neither Anza's nor Solana's program documentation explicitly covers on-chain scalar multiplication of confidential ciphertexts or its compute cost, so this route needs engineering validation before anyone commits to it. xStock collateral cannot join without Backed's cooperation, because its mints require Backed's manual approval and confidential balances would still reveal which stock a user holds through the token account's mint. The Token-2022 source suggests the permanent delegate cannot reach confidential balances ([token-2022 source](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/main/program/src/extension/confidential_transfer/processor.rs)), which gives Backed a reason to resist; that reading comes from the code and needs confirmation from the maintainers.

Liquidation is where privacy pays most and where Mercury's position helps. Public positions are a hit list: one study counted 31,057 liquidations yielding $89.18M of liquidator profit over 28 months, with destructive front-running among liquidators ([Qin et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.05511)), and a security writer reconstructed one person's full card history from public Gnosis Pay addresses and warned of physical-attack risk ([tzedonn](https://tzedonn.substack.com/p/22-crypto-cards-will-get-you-robbed)). As sole lender, Mercury can run liquidation itself and sell seized collateral through sealed or Dutch auctions, so outside bidders see a lot and never the borrower's book. Leakage survives even perfect encryption, since liquidation events, lot sizes, top-up timing and oracle-driven batch timing stay public, Zama and Fhenix both concede timing and metadata leakage, and Penumbra's batching design admits that a single-swap batch offers no protection ([Penumbra](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/flow.html)). Batched liquidations and batched top-ups reduce that linkability without removing it.

The price of this model is that Mercury becomes custodian of visibility. A Mercury key that decrypts every position is a target in the same way an upgrade key is, and it needs the same discipline of HSM storage, logged rotation, and separation between the operational keys that compute health and the keys that grant regulators access. For regulator access, threshold or MPC custody of decryption rights and user-chosen conditional escrow are the strongest patterns; Arcium's model lets data owned by its MPC execution environment be decrypted only collectively by its nodes, under a dishonest-majority assumption, and re-encrypts a value to a chosen recipient through a step it calls sealing ([Arcium](https://docs.arcium.com/developers/encryption)). Arcium-computed health is the only route in the research where no single party sees the book, and it depends on Arcium's node operators and on C-SPL reaching mainnet.

### Card privacy is decided by funding topology, and just-in-time funding publishes every swipe

Card rails expose everything to the parties who run them. The network sees the card number, merchant name and location, category code, date and amount ([Mastercard](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/press/2022/september/how-we-support-merchants-and-consumers-through-clear-consistent-principles/)), and network tokens hide the card number from merchants but not from the issuer ([Evervault](https://docs.evervault.com/cards/network-tokens.md)). The privacy problem specific to crypto cards is on-chain linkability. Dune's card-transactions dataset already indexes user addresses, dollar amounts and timestamps for card spends and top-ups across EVM chains and Solana, including Rain-issued programs ([Dune](https://docs.dune.com/data-catalog/curated/payments/card-transactions)), and Bridge's webhook returns an on-chain transaction hash for each authorisation pull ([Bridge docs](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/cards/overview/noncustodial.md)). Under Bridge's non-custodial model, every swipe therefore becomes a public event whose timing and amount can be matched against merchant receipts.

Inco's taxonomy frames the trade: custodial cards put nothing on-chain, self-custodial cards publish each user-to-program transfer, and on-chain settlement programs expose the issuer's own volumes ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/crypto-card-architecture-custody-settlement-privacy)). Mercury's options rank the same way. Per-user pulls at authorisation are the most linkable; a confidential card sub-account swept to a settlement pool on a fixed schedule hides amounts but still leaks timing and count; and a pooled settlement account removes the per-wallet link entirely while making Mercury, or Bridge through its custodial Bridge Wallet, custodian of the spend float. A capped middle path fits a user-controlled account, in which the user pre-authorises batched, fixed-denomination top-ups from the account into a Mercury card pool, authorisations debit the pool's ledger rather than the chain, custody is limited to the float, and the public learns only that a wallet funds a Mercury card. Bridge's documented non-custodial model does not support that pattern, so it implies Bridge's custodial option or a different issuer arrangement. Two smaller leaks deserve design attention. A credit line that draws on each swipe publishes spending behaviour through borrow events, and sub-accounts seeded only by owner and index can be derived by anyone who knows the owner's address, so separating card funds from investing funds buys little privacy unless the seeds include a secret. The one ZK-note card in the survey, Payy, shows how early that end of the market is: L2BEAT flags no delay on its code upgrades and a TVL of about $711 ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/payy)).

### Identity stays off-chain while the chain carries an eligibility flag

The Solana Attestation Service has run on mainnet since May 2025, with Civic, Range and Sumsub among its users ([Range](https://www.range.org/blog/introducing-solana-attestation-service)); its attestations are signed data stored directly on-chain with expiry but no ZK or privacy features ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tools/attestations/overview)). That suits a public, PII-free flag recording that a wallet passed a given Mercury KYC tier and residency bucket until a given date, which Mercury's program can check at account opening and on sensitive instructions, and it is the wrong place for identity data. Mature ZK identity sits mostly outside Solana. Human ID's Clean Hands credential pairs a ZK proof of verified, unsanctioned status with name and date of birth encrypted to a network, under decryption conditions set by a contract the user selects ([Human ID](https://docs.id.human.tech/architecture/clean-hands)); the escrow exists because purely anonymous verification leaves businesses unable to meet AML duties. Regulators are curious but unmoved. SEC Commissioner Peirce endorsed attribute-based credentials in a personal speech in September 2026 and left the SEC weeks later, while US customer identification rules still require name, date of birth, address and ID number ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/sec-peirce-backs-zero-knowledge-proofs-for-kyc/)). The realistic model is full KYC held by Mercury, a minimal on-chain flag, and ZK credentials only for third-party integrations that should learn only that a user is eligible, not who the user is. Duplication remains a practical problem, since Bridge runs its own KYC for card endorsement; Gnosis Pay shows one way through by letting partners reuse their Sumsub KYC ([Gnosis Pay](https://docs.gnosispay.com/concepts/architecture.md)).

Keys are the hidden dependency in every confidential design. Solana's default derivation signs a fixed message and derives one ElGamal key and one AES key from the signature, so nothing is stored, but sharing those keys discloses every confidential account the user has ([Solana integration guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/integration-guide)). Signature-based derivation works only if signing is deterministic. Privy reconstitutes sharded keys inside trusted execution environments ([Privy](https://privy.io/blog/how-privy-embedded-wallets-work)), the research found no Privy statement guaranteeing deterministic Solana signatures, and threshold signing schemes typically use fresh nonces. The same guide's `fromIkm` and passkey `fromPrf` routes decouple viewing keys from the signing key, at the cost of a Mercury-run recovery copy. Letting a user share a statement without exposing everything requires per-account derived keys, or a Mercury-signed statement backed by equality proofs against the on-chain ciphertexts. No source published Solana proof-generation times on mobile, so a benchmark on mid-range Android has to come before any privacy UX commitment. Risk teams and auditors need aggregates rather than per-user data, and threshold-decrypted totals plus a Merkle-sum liabilities commitment with a non-negativity proof both fit; Ethereum's PSE team ended its Summa proof-of-liabilities work after concluding that cryptographic cost was a small part of the problem, and that completeness and reserve ownership are the hard parts ([PSE](https://pse.dev/blog/retrospective-summa)).

## Every module and privacy layer needs controls Mercury must own

### Four failure classes recur across account designs

Nearly every loss in the surveyed account and card systems falls into one of four classes. The first is health-check bypass, where a new or edge-case instruction changes balances without passing the check. Euler's `donateToReserves` skipped its solvency check in 2023 and let an attacker self-liquidate a deliberately insolvent position for about $197M ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/euler-finance-incident-the-largest-hack-of-2023)), and in 2025 marginfi's new `transfer_to_new_account` ignored the flash-loan flag, letting liabilities move to an empty account that then passed its end-of-loan check and putting more than $160M at risk before a bounty caught it ([Asymmetric Research](https://asymmetric.com/threat-contained-marginfi-flash-loan-vulnerability/)). The second is forged authorisation in the permission layer. In June 2026 Gnosis Pay's Zodiac modules read a reverted call's return data without checking that the call had succeeded, so about $1.5M was taken and about $300k made inaccessible across 5,281 wallets ([Gnosis Pay](https://gnosispay.com/blog/post-mortem-gnosis-pay-vulnerability-exploit)); the bug entered in 2023, and its fix landed only in a separately maintained fork while deployed instances still compiled against the legacy package ([Verichains](https://blog.verichains.io/p/gnosis-pay-exploit-the-devs-discovered)). Rain's Ed25519 offset reuse two months later belongs to the same class. The third is admin capture. In April 2026 Drift's 2-of-5 Squads multisig with no timelock signed durable-nonce transactions under false pretences, after which the attacker listed a fake collateral token with a controlled oracle and raised withdrawal guards to absurd levels, taking about $285M from roughly $309M of TVL; as the 4Pillars post-mortem put it, "the margin system worked as designed, but every input had been manipulated" ([4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/reflections-on-the-drift-protocol-exploit)). The fourth is oracle and input manipulation, as when Mango's token was pumped about 13-fold on the venues feeding its oracle in 2022 and losses exceeded $110M ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8647-23)).

Each class has a matching control, and together they form the account's security specification. Against bypass, every handler that mutates a Mercury Account should return through one finalisation function that runs health and invariant checks, with continuous integration failing any instruction that skips it; deferred checks should follow Mango's whitelist of permitted inner instructions rather than marginfi's blanket skip; transfers, closes and migrations should be blocked while a region or flash state is open; and invariant fuzzing should test that no instruction sequence ends with lower health and more outflow. Against forged authorisation, any inspection of Ed25519 or secp256k1 precompile instructions must confirm that offsets and instruction indices point at the instruction itself, delegation should live in Mercury's own program logic rather than in a generic, upgradeable third-party module framework, Swig or Squads versions in front of it should be pinned and their security fixes tracked, and Mercury's caps should sit in Mercury's program so a smart-account bug cannot exceed them. Every deployment must be upgraded together, because Rain lost money through a stale version. Against admin capture, Drift's post-mortem recommends timelocks of at least 48 hours on admin transfer and new listings, capped weights for newly listed collateral with separate timelocks on weight changes, bounded withdrawal guards, and human-readable signing with real-time alerts ([4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/reflections-on-the-drift-protocol-exploit)); Mercury should add hard-coded outflow ceilings the admin cannot lift instantly and a ban on durable-nonce pre-signing for privileged actions. Solana's leading lenders show how wide practice still varies, from Kamino's 5-of-10 multisig with a 24-hour timelock ([Solscan](https://solscan.io/account/6hhBGCtmg7tPWUSgp3LG6X2rsmYWAc4tNsA6G4CnfQbM)) to marginfi's 7-of-15 with none ([Solscan](https://solscan.io/account/7FCPipJWVbPbdHymVt1gJYwKciakkJz5GahdQySemvHk)). Against input manipulation, the controls are per-asset caps, conservative weights, session-aware oracle bounds for xStocks, and the extension checks in the collateral registry.

The delegate table should combine the best of the surveyed designs: Gearbox's smallest-possible bitmask, Coinbase's per-period caps, Gnosis Pay's pinned destination, an expiry on every grant, and Aave v4's separation of Giver actions (deposit, repay) from Taker actions (withdraw, borrow, spend). The user should also get an EVC-style lockdown that cancels every delegation except repayment and liquidation, which limits the damage from a compromised relayer, keeper, card service or AI agent. Governance overrides of user permissions, which Gearbox uses to add partial liquidation, should be confined to liquidation-type actions.

### Privacy options each carry a compliance price

| Privacy option                                        | Hides from the public                      | Status                                             | Compliance controls it needs                                                                                                                                        | Security and liveness controls                                                             |

| MPC-computed health (Arcium, C-SPL)                   | Positions, even from any single key holder | Alpha; C-SPL unconfirmed                           | Sealed disclosure to Mercury and regulators under defined conditions                                                                                                | Dishonest-majority trust in node operators; liveness fallback                              |

The perimeter screens the parts of these designs that move money. Umbra verifies a Range risk score on-chain through a Switchboard oracle before transfers, claims and withdrawals proceed, scopes viewing keys by date range or transaction, and launched with a $500 deposit cap ([Range](https://range.org/blog/umbra-integrates-range-to-power-real-time-risk-and-compliance-for-private-transfers-on-solana); [Arcium](https://arcium.substack.com/p/umbra-is-now-open-to-the-public-powered)), while Cloak screens at both entry and exit ([Range](https://range.org/blog/how-cloak-screens-for-sanctioned-addresses-before-its-privacy-layer-applies)). Laundering risk is bounded mainly by keeping the system closed, through an attestation check at account opening, sanctions re-screening for the life of the account, caps on shielded flows, and flows Mercury can see; open-ended shielded sends to arbitrary wallets are where risk concentrates, so sends leaving Mercury's domain should either unshield or pass through a screened pool. The demand evidence counsels modesty, since Privacy Pools held about $9.25M after more than a year on Ethereum ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools)).

The legal frame tolerates accountable privacy and rejects anonymity. From July 2027, Article 79 of the EU's Anti-Money Laundering Regulation bars CASPs from keeping accounts that allow anonymisation of the holder or increased obfuscation of transactions ([Springlex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-79/)), and whether privacy from the public that stays visible to the CASP counts as increased obfuscation is unresolved, a question counsel should answer for EU users before that date. Travel-rule messages must carry amounts off-chain, because confidential transfers hide them from counterparty VASPs. In the US, Roman Storm's conviction for conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business still stands, with a retrial on other counts set for April 2027 ([Crypto Valley Journal](https://cryptovalleyjournal.com/focus/legal-and-compliance/tornado-cash-roman-storm-trial-delayed-to-april-2027/)), so any relaying function in a privacy feature needs money-transmission analysis first. Hidden insolvency is the risk that privacy adds and public DeFi lacks, and because Mercury holds the credit risk it is mainly a supervisory issue, answered by the aggregate disclosures above plus a liabilities commitment each user can check for their own position. Every privacy layer also adds a liveness dependency on its exit path, whether an MPC cluster, a TEE operator, the proof program or the key escrow, and each needs a documented fallback to a public exit or a governed unshield.

## The sequencing choices turn on what cannot be undone

Some of Mercury's decisions are cheap to revisit and others fix the shape of everything after them, and the second group deserves attention first.

The container has three defensible paths. A wallet-first path keeps earn receipts in Privy wallets and funds the card from the wallet's USDC through Bridge's documented flow, with a Privy policy blocking other outbound transfers; it ships fastest, but its card protection is an off-chain policy, credit cannot be bound to the card, and moving cards later means reprovisioning delegates. An account-first path puts every dollar under the Mercury program from launch, which avoids later migration but maximises the custody core's blast radius before the core has a record. A hybrid keeps idle cash and earn shares in the wallet and opens a Mercury credit account PDA the first time a user activates the card or borrows, so the card always points at a program-owned buffer, the custody core holds only funds that need enforcement, and the EVC's one-controller rule maps onto one credit sub-account per user; its weakness is the UX of money in two places presented as one balance. The strongest argument against any Mercury-owned container comes from Rain and Avici: an issuer-run collateral contract outsources the hardest code, and Avici showed that it can still fail and that the app still pays.

The card choice pulls privacy and credit in opposite directions. Bridge's just-in-time model aligns with Privy and Stripe, keeps funds self-custodial and survives an issuer failure, as Kulipa's cardholders found, but it publishes every swipe and pulls only from USDC. Credit-model issuers take real-time stock pricing out of Mercury's authorisation path and earn more interchange, but they bring consumer-credit duties for EU users from November 2026 and expect to run their own collateral contract. Neither removes the need for a second provider, since Bridge's EU and APAC coverage is still only planned and issuer failure is a matter of when, not whether.

Privacy can run on a parallel track ordered by maturity, and its cheapest gains come from topology rather than cryptography: sub-account seeds outsiders cannot derive, batched or pooled card settlement where the issuer model allows it, MEV-protected swap submission, and protocol-run liquidation auctions that never expose a borrower's book. The next layer turns on a decision that has to be made early, because confidential transfers must be initialised when any Mercury-issued vault share or dollar mint is created, with a Mercury auditor key and a public exit path, and then switched on for KYC'd users once the Privy key flow and auditor indexing exist. Party privacy for sends can follow through a screened partner pool behind caps once that partner has audited mainnet code, private collateral with Mercury-held keys after engineering validation of the proof path, and trustless private health only after C-SPL ships. Several gates carry dates: consumer-credit rules for EU card credit from 20 November 2026, Bridge's planned EU and APAC rollout during 2026, CPI depth of eight at an unset date after Agave 4.4, and AMLR Article 79 in July 2027. Every module Mercury launches before July 2027 should be designed so its privacy posture can be explained to an EU supervisor as accountable rather than obfuscating.

## Conclusion

The card, not earn, decides Mercury's architecture. Earn alone works with tokens in the user's wallet and one-signature withdrawals, but the moment users spend against stocks the protocol must hold borrowed dollars and pending captures where the user cannot move them, which is Gearbox's credit account and, on Solana, a program-owned PDA with one health check. That reframes self-custody: users keeping their Privy keys stays necessary but stops being sufficient, because the real guarantee becomes the upgrade discipline of a small core program and the permission layer around it, which is exactly where 2026's card losses occurred. The structure that makes the products easy also concentrates risk in one program, so its immutability, timelocks and delegate rules are product decisions rather than engineering details.

Privacy and the card pull against each other, since the most self-custodial card funding publishes every swipe and the most private funding is custodial, so card privacy is really a choice of issuer model and funding topology. Mercury's position as sole, KYC'd lender turns out to be an advantage. It converts private credit from an open cryptography problem into a key-custody problem that can be solved years before trustless private lending exists anywhere, at the price of making Mercury the custodian of visibility. A Mercury that can see every position has to guard the keys that see as carefully as the keys that move funds, and it should promise users privacy from the public, never privacy from Mercury.
