# Rent the Mint, Own the Float

Mercury can put a Mercury-branded dollar on Solana within weeks by renting a regulated issuer such as Bridge, Paxos, Anchorage, Agora, Ethena or Coinbase. What it would own is the brand and a share of reserve income, not the mint. On every issuer-run Token-2022 dollar checked on-chain (CASH, USDG, PYUSD, AUSD, USDPT), the issuer keeps the freeze, permanent-delegate, confidential-transfer and transfer-hook authorities. The prize is the float. Reserves earn roughly **2.2–3.3% gross** depending on the cash buffer, or **$2.2–3.3M a year per $100M** of idle balances before an undisclosed issuer fee. That is three to six times what a 10% lending take rate earns on the same money. Yet wallet-branded dollars have stalled in the tens of millions (JupUSD $43M, USDPT $19M), and Hyperliquid's USDH was sunset, so revenue will track balances held inside Mercury rather than adoption outside it. Open-loop programs such as OUSD and USDG already pay "almost all" reserve income to apps that never brand a coin. The rules shape the earn product more than the code does: US proposals under the GENIUS Act presume a violation when a white-label brand pays yield to holders of its own coin, and MiCA Article 50 bars interest-equivalents in the EU, so savings belong in an opt-in wrapper funded by real borrower interest, never in the base token. For credit, every major venue lends dollars from pools against stock collateral. The two dollars minted against equities are small or have lost their peg, and minting against xStocks would require a second Mercury token sitting above the regulated coin. Depth for a new dollar comes from two to four market makers with redemption at par, not from incentives. Privacy in 2026 concretely means hiding amounts, which Solana's confidential transfers now do in a single transaction. Hiding who pays whom needs capped partner pools or Mercury's own ledger, and it runs into the EU's July 2027 ban on accounts that allow "increased obfuscation". The sequence that fits is to distribute an existing dollar, then brand one, then build lending on it. Stock-backed minting and private balances stay as gated options, and both depend on Mercury controlling a token layer it cannot rent.

## Renting an issuer buys the brand and the float, not the mint

### Five routes exist, and only the rented ones ship in weeks

On Solana, a branded dollar can launch today through five live routes: **Bridge Open Issuance** (Phantom's CASH and Open Standard's OUSD), **Paxos** (USDG, with PayPal's PYUSD as the white-label template), **Anchorage Digital Bank** (Western Union's USDPT), **Ethena Whitelabel** (Jupiter's JupUSD) and **Coinbase Custom Stablecoins** (Flipcash's USDC-backed USDF). M0 and Agora also run on Solana, with far smaller footprints there. None of the eight major issuance platforms publishes fees, minimums or a standard reserve split, and only Bridge publishes a launch timeline ([Upshift](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/stablecoin-issuance-platforms)). Bridge onboarding runs through sales. A custom coin can be set up in "as little as one day" when no new chain is needed. The brand picks the name, ticker, logo, reserve mix and open- or closed-loop design, but "you do not self-mint", because Bridge operates mint and burn ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/how-to-issue-a-branded-stablecoin-with-bridge-open-issuance/), secondary).

Building a licensed issuer instead takes most of a year or more. An EU e-money institution needs €350k of capital and typically 6–12 months ([Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/stablecoin-issuer-licence-2/)). Hong Kong licensed only two of 36 applicants in its first round, at HK$25M minimum paid-up capital ([Stablecoin Laws](https://stablecoinlaws.org/2026/02/02/hkma-stablecoin-licensing-2026-timeline-requirements-reserves-redemption-rules); [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/stablecoin-law/)). Even established issuers are waiting. The OCC conditionally approved national trust charters for Paxos (December 2025), Bridge (February 2026) and Agora (September 2026), but only BitGo and Circle had opened as live banks by one October 2026 count ([The Industry Spread](https://theindustryspread.com/occ-five-crypto-trust-charters-two-opened/)).

Who the legal issuer is matters more than the logo. Bridge's coins are "currently issued by Bridge Building Inc." under US state licences, and Bridge's national trust bank "does not currently issue"; its EEA business runs through Bridge Building S.A. in Luxembourg, which the CSSF authorises as both a crypto-asset service provider and an e-money institution ([Bridge blog](https://bridge.xyz/blog/ousd-is-live-issued-by-bridge)). USDG comes from a Singapore Major Payments Institution supervised by the MAS and from a MiCA-compliant entity supervised in Finland ([Global Dollar Network](https://globaldollar.com/)), and Agora issues AUSD from Bermuda ([Agora](https://www.agora.finance/product/white-label)). Bridge has a stack advantage for Mercury. Privy, Mercury's wallet provider, is "a Stripe company" ([PR Newswire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260306to03146/sky-savings-rate-now-available-to-all-developers-building-on-privy-a-stripe-company)), Bridge is Stripe's issuing arm ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/crypto/2025/10/01/stripe-crypto-stablecoins-open-issuance-bridge-blockchain-tempo/)), and Phantom's CASH already proves the route on Solana. Paxos's non-US issuing entities are, however, the cleaner jurisdictional fit for a user base outside the US, for reasons the legal discussion below makes clear.

### The issuer keeps the keys that matter

On-chain checks on 21 October 2026 found the same Token-2022 setup on every issuer-run dollar. CASH carries a **permanent delegate** (one key able to move or burn tokens in any holder's account), a mint-close authority, an initialised **confidential-transfer** configuration with `autoApproveNewAccounts=false` and no auditor key, and a **transfer-hook** slot with no program set. USDG and PYUSD share one address for freeze authority and permanent delegate, and add a 0-bps transfer-fee configuration. USDPT adds a pausable switch ([Solscan: CASH](https://solscan.io/token/CASHx9KJUStyftLFWGvEVf59SGeG9sh5FfcnZMVPCASH); [Solscan: USDG](https://solscan.io/token/2u1tszSeqZ3qBWF3uNGPFc8TzMk2tdiwknnRMWGWjGWH); [Solscan: PYUSD](https://solscan.io/token/2b1kV6DkPAnxd5ixfnxCpjxmKwqjjaYmCZfHsFu24GXo); [Solscan: USDPT](https://solscan.io/token/HVWf8JmLoHs99Lw8Psf3fyqAtA4crWxCPkrmSdNjhNH3)). JupUSD is the outlier: a classic SPL mint with none of these extensions, which suggests Jupiter valued compatibility with DeFi protocols over issuer features ([Solscan: JupUSD](https://solscan.io/token/JuprjznTrTSp2UFa3ZBUFgwdAmtZCq4MQCwysN55USD)). No provider documents letting a brand hold or co-hold any of these authorities.

That cuts three ways. **First, Mercury could not change issuer without the issuer's cooperation.** The one clean swap, Ethena moving USDtb's US issuance to Anchorage, worked because the token contract stayed put ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/07/24/ethena-taps-anchorage-to-issue-usd1-5b-usdtb-stablecoin-in-u-s-under-genius-act)). Every other exit has meant redeeming holders and migrating them into another coin. New York's regulator ordered Paxos to stop minting BUSD in February 2023 ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/consumers/alerts/paxos_and_binance)). USDH was retired in May 2026 with feeless redemption to USDC, and only its brand assets went to Coinbase ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/401233/coinbase-hyperliquid-official-deployer-usdc)).

A wrapper over swappable backing is more portable but carries its own risks. M0 "extensions" are brand tokens backed 1:1 by $M, in treasury, holder-yield and whitelisted variants ([M0 docs](https://docs.m0.org/protocol/extensions)). On Solana, though, $M is frozen by default, its index updates depend on an Ethereum hub and a Wormhole-based bridge, and holder yield depends on an off-chain distribution process ([M0 docs](https://docs.m0.org/protocol/solana)). Ethena lets partners rebalance backing among USDe, USDtb and other approved stablecoins ([Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/overview/ethena-whitelabel)), but USDe's supply fell from about $14.7B to $6.3B within two months of its October 2025 price dislocation ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/news/usde-capitalisation-by-ethena-plummets-by-8-3-billion-since-october)).

**Second, renting the mint keeps the power to seize funds off Mercury's books.** US money-transmission analysis turns on "total independent control", and a co-signer that "cannot unilaterally access or move" funds is not a transmitter ([Covington on FinCEN 2019](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2019/10/fincen_issues_guidance_to_synthesize_regulatory_framework_for_virtual_currency.pdf)). A Mercury-held permanent delegate would hand Mercury exactly that power over every holder.

**Third, the issuer setup leaves doors open.** Confidential transfers must be configured when the mint is created and cannot be added later ([SPL docs](https://www.solana-program.com/docs/confidential-balances)), but the issuer setup already includes them. The hook slot can probably be filled later by whoever holds the hook authority, without migrating the mint. Whether Mercury gets its own auditor key or hook program is therefore a contract term, not an engineering barrier, and it should be settled before the mint exists.

### Revenue follows balances held in the app, not the logo

Bridge's quoted reserve rates set the economics (secondary source): the Treasury portion earns the effective fed funds rate minus 20 bps, off-chain cash earns that rate minus 200 bps, and stablecoins held as reserves earn nothing. Bridge suggests starting with about 20% cash. At $100M of supply, its illustration gives about **2.2% with a 50/50 cash/Treasury split and 3.3% with 10/90**. Bridge "shares the majority of treasury rewards with the issuer, minus issuance fees", and pays monthly ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/how-to-issue-a-branded-stablecoin-with-bridge-open-issuance/)). Phantom's CASH holds 7.7% of its reserves in cash ([Bridge reserves](https://reserves.bridge.xyz/cash)), so the low-buffer end is achievable.

That is roughly **$33k a year per $1M of float**, or $2.2–3.3M per $100M. A lending pool with a 10% reserve factor ([Jupiter Lend FAQ](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/lend/faq.md)), 7–8% borrow rates and 70–80% utilisation earns its operator about $0.5–0.6M on the same $100M. Idle float is therefore worth three to six times more to Mercury than running the lending market. Bridge's fee is undisclosed, so the net figure is unknown.

The catch is scale. Phantom's CASH, the best-performing wallet coin, reached **127.8M** after a year ([Solscan: CASH](https://solscan.io/token/CASHx9KJUStyftLFWGvEVf59SGeG9sh5FfcnZMVPCASH)). JupUSD sits at **42.9M**, on a venue that handles billions ([Solscan: JupUSD](https://solscan.io/token/JuprjznTrTSp2UFa3ZBUFgwdAmtZCq4MQCwysN55USD)), and Western Union's USDPT at 18.7M.

Hyperliquid's USDH is the starkest case. Native Markets won Hyperliquid's 2025 contest to issue USDH, the most contested issuer award of that year, with a bid of 50% of reserve income to buybacks and 50% to growth. It beat rivals that offered 95–100% ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/paxos-frax-agora-and-native-markets-compete-to-build-usdh-stablecoin/)). USDH then stalled near $100M against about $5B of USDC on the same venue and was sunset in May 2026 ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbase-becomes-hyperliquids-official-usdc-treasury-deployer-as-usdh-sunsets/)).

The dollars that scaled came with exchange or payments distribution: USDG at 645M and PYUSD at 682M on Solana, and USD1 at 1.4B ([Solscan: USD1](https://solscan.io/token/USD1ttGY1N17NEEHLmELoaybftRBUSErhqYiQzvEmuB)). Distribution, not ownership, sets revenue. Even Circle sent about **59%** of its H1 2026 revenue ($819M of about $1.4B) to distribution partners and transaction costs, keeping about seven cents of net profit per revenue dollar ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/circle-pays-819-million-usdc-partners/)).

The economic gap between branding a coin and distributing someone else's has narrowed to roughly the issuance fee. OUSD pays "almost all" reserve yield to distributors pro rata to the supply they put into circulation, offers partners equity, and charges no mint or redemption fees ([Bridge blog](https://bridge.xyz/blog/ousd-is-live-issued-by-bridge)). Paying distributors by the supply they originate suits a self-custodial app, because Mercury mints on its users' behalf when they on-ramp. CASH partners "each earn on the reserves for supply they originate", per a Stripe customer story; this wording comes from a search snippet and was not independently confirmed ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/es-us/customers/phantom-spotlight)). Meanwhile a branded coin starts with no liquidity, whereas USDG already holds within 6 bps of par on a $5M sale.

The case for a Mercury-branded dollar therefore rests on control, brand and an exclusive ticker, not on better economics. It also adds an operating chore. Jupiter's token search returns many unverified pump.fun tokens using the OUSD and USDF tickers ([Jupiter token API](https://lite-api.jup.ag/tokens/v2/search?query=USDF)), so every integration must hard-code the genuine mint address.

### US and EU rules make Mercury a party to the yield ban

A brand does not escape the yield rules; it pulls Mercury into them. The OCC's GENIUS proposal (published March 2026) adds a rebuttable presumption of violation when an issuer arranges for a "related third party" to pay holders yield. Related third parties expressly include white-label and co-brand partners, and wallets offering yield "as a service". Profit-sharing with a non-affiliate white-label partner is not itself banned, but commentary says those profits "likely cannot be passed on", and the ban reaches OCC-registered foreign issuers too ([Ashurst Perkins Coie](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/stablecoin-interest-yield-and-rewards-occ-proposes-sweeping-regulations-under-the-genius-act/)).

The Federal Reserve's proposal, published 29 September 2026 with comments closing 30 November, mirrors that presumption and also asks whether issuers should be limited to a single brand because of cross-brand run risk ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/29/2026-19860/implementing-the-federal-reserve-boards-responsibilities-under-the-genius-act)).

GENIUS takes effect on the earlier of 18 January 2027 or 120 days after final rules ([Upshift](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/stablecoin-issuance-platforms)). The Senate's May 2026 CLARITY compromise would allow activity-based rewards, including for "providing liquidity in DeFi protocols", but its enactment is unconfirmed ([Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2026/05/05/the-clarity-acts-yield-compromise-what-the-senate-actually-agreed-to-and-why-it-matters/)).

Neither proposal reviewed carves out non-US holders. A Mercury dollar issued by a US entity would therefore likely bring Mercury into the presumption wherever its users live. Whether issuing from Paxos Singapore, Bridge Building S.A. or Agora Bermuda changes that is a question for counsel.

In the EU, MiCA Article 48 lets a non-issuer offer an e-money token only with the issuer's written consent, and the offeror must then comply with Articles 50 and 53 ([MiCA Art. 48](https://satoshishrine.com/celex/32023R1114/ART_48/20260823-32c6d21e)). Article 50 bars issuers and crypto-asset service providers from granting interest or any benefit tied to how long a token is held ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-50-prohibition-granting-interest)). The 2026 review points toward tighter rules. The EBA wants the ban kept and flags lending of e-money tokens as an arbitrage risk ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/12cb4f01-0d51-46bf-a561-165ac486698f/EBA%20response%20to%20EC%20targeted%20consultation%20on%20MiCA%20review.pdf)). The European System of Central Banks wants it widened to crypto lending, borrowing and staking that replicate interest ([MiCA Crypto Alliance](https://www.micacryptoalliance.com/news/europes-central-banks-tell-brussels-to-widen-the-stablecoin-interest-ban-and-close-the-defi-gap)).

The industry's standard structure is a business-to-business reserve share to the app, which the app passes on as discretionary, revocable "rewards". Kraken, for example, sets its USDG rewards "in its sole discretion" ([Kraken](https://blog.kraken.com/news/earn-usdg-rewards)). That is precisely the structure both the US proposals and the EU review are now scrutinising.

## Savings and stock-backed credit work only when real cash flows pay for them

### Every surviving savings rate is paid from float, borrowers or basis

Sky is the clearest evidence of where savings margins come from. At end-June 2026, about $10.04B of USDS generated **$419M of annualised gross revenue, about 4.2%**. The governance-set savings rate of 3.60–3.80% went to roughly $4.4B in sUSDS, 44% of supply ([Sky June 2026 update](https://insights.skyeco.com/insights/sky-ecosystem-june-financial-operational-update); [Coinfomania](https://coinfomania.com/sky-savings-rate-rises-to-3-80-enhancing-susds-capital/)). The other 56% of USDS cost Sky nothing, and that free float produced most of a **37.3% net margin**. Even so, Sky's loss reserve stood at only $82.5M against a $150M target, about 0.8% of supply, and governance diverted buybacks to rebuild it ([Sky June 2026 update](https://insights.skyeco.com/insights/sky-ecosystem-june-financial-operational-update)).

Other owned dollars fund savings from borrowers or from trading basis. Aave's GHO pays a fixed 4.50% on 158.6M of sGHO under a stated rule that savings cost must stay below lending interest plus backing yield plus swap fees, and the Aave DAO keeps only about $3.0M a year ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/gho-stewards-august-2026-gho-borrow-rate-and-aave-savings-rate-update/25534)). Liquity v2 sends 75% of borrower interest to its Stability Pool ([Liquity docs](https://docs.liquity.org/v2-faq/bold-and-earn)). Ethena sends 0% of ongoing revenue to its reserve fund ([Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/protocol-overview/reserve-fund.md)), which held only $62.6M against $4.76B of USDe in October 2026 while sUSDe paid about 4.76% ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/marktberichte/usde-supply-closes-at-4-94-billion-usd-what-it-means-for-the-ethena-usde-price/70358853)).

Savings rates cluster at **3.6–4.8%**, at or just above tokenized Treasury yields of about 3.8% ([DefiLlama: USTB](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/1910847a-f8b5-40ce-a1ab-1dafdded5fbb)). That sets a hard competitive floor for Mercury. Jupiter Lend's USDC Earn paid **5.62%** on 21 October 2026 ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/d783c8df-e2ed-44b4-8317-161ccc1b5f06)). An earn product on the Mercury dollar that paid only reserve yield (about 3.3% before fees) would lose to a user swapping into USDC and lending it there.

Jupiter's JupUSD is the closest working template, and it shows both the design and its limits. JupUSD itself pays nothing. Savers hold JUICED, a Jupiter Lend vault share whose yield blends borrow interest on lent JupUSD with Treasury yield from JupUSD's reserves, routed through Jupiter's rewards distributor. The reserve yield is spread across all JUICED shares, so per-share yield dilutes if the vault grows larger than minted supply ([Jupiter docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/jupusd/juiced)).

The blend works at small scale: JupUSD Earn paid 7.25% on $47.3M on 21 October 2026, against 5.62% for USDC on the same platform, though how that rate breaks down is not disclosed ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/0dbb5021-00c7-40e2-9e2b-a757f4305ebf)). The dollar beneath it is losing ground, though. Over Q3 2026, JupUSD supply fell 12% to 45.1M. The planned conversion of Jupiter's perps liquidity pool from USDC into JupUSD never happened; the pool held $18.7M of JupUSD against $127M of USDC. Unwinding the leveraged JUICED Loop product cost a **$24.3M reserve drawdown** ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)).

Two lessons follow for a Mercury earn product. **First, it should be an opt-in wrapper.** Users would hold a share token backed by Mercury dollars lent to xStock borrowers, with reserve income flowing in only where the law allows. That reserve share is exactly what the US related-third-party presumption targets if the issuer is a US entity. Borrower interest is legally different, because it is lending yield. Even there, large US platforms chose user-directed lending from the user's own wallet through a third-party curator; Robinhood Earn, for instance, routes USDG into Morpho and Spark ([Raptor Group](https://www.raptorgroup.com/news/paxos-spark-powers-robinhoods-usdg-stablecoin-savings-product-launch/)). The custodial alternative is what the SEC fined BlockFi $100M for ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2022-26)).

**Second, earn cannibalises the float.** If half of supply sat in earn at Treasury yields minus 50 bps, savings would cost about 1.6–1.7% of total supply, leaving about 2% before operating, custody and issuer costs. Every additional dollar moved into savings turns Mercury's highest-margin revenue into a pass-through to users.

### Pools, not mints, carry stock-backed credit today

In October 2026, stock-backed credit means lending USDC from a pool. Aave V4's Equities Hub on Base, launched 25 September 2026, accepts seven Coinbase stock tokens as collateral for USDC loans only, for non-US users. Collateral factors (the share of collateral value that can be borrowed) run 65–79%, the borrow cap is about $21M, and GHO is only "planned" as a future borrowable asset ([Aave](https://aave.com/blog/coinbase-tokenized-stocks); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-09-25-aave-v4-on-base-adds-coinbase-tokenized-stocks-as-collateral-for-usdc-loans-416372)). Kamino, Jupiter Lend and Venus use the same pool model.

Usage is small. About 42% of tokenized-stock supply is eligible as DeFi collateral, but only **2.6%** is actually borrowed against. Across SPYx, NVDAx and TSLAx, lending holds about $24.5M against $577M of perpetual-futures open interest ([RedStone](https://www.redstone.finance/research/tokenized-stocks-2026)).

Only two cases of a dollar minted against stocks exist. Falcon Finance began accepting xStocks to mint its USDf in October 2025 ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/falcon-finance-and-backed-pioneer-onchain-yield-from-tokenized-stocks)), after USDf had already fallen to $0.9783 that July amid collateral-quality concerns, with only $5.51M of on-chain liquidity ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/falcon-usd-stablecoin-depegs-liquidity-collateral-concerns)). Frankencoin, a Swiss-franc stablecoin, holds about $4.2M of Ondo SPYon in positions that need no price oracle, but its liquidations take days ([RedStone](https://www.redstone.finance/research/tokenized-stocks-2026); [Frankencoin docs](https://docs.frankencoin.com/positions)). The earlier stock-minting systems are gone: Terra's Mirror died with Terra, and Synthetix retired its stock synths.

Handling of closed-market hours has converged on a shared rule set. Aave holds the last price on weekends, so "health factors can decline only through interest accrual", and pauses a market on a stock split until the token multiplier updates ([Aave](https://aave.com/blog/coinbase-tokenized-stocks)). Kamino accepts off-hours prices only within a band around the last close. Jupiter Lend pauses at session transitions and 24 hours before corporate actions, freezes NVDAx and TSLAx around earnings, and blocks actions on prices more than 10 minutes stale ([Jupiter docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/lend/borrow/xstocks)). These rules matter because US equities trade only **32.5 of 168 weekly hours**. One pre-IPO perpetual-futures venue misread a 5-for-1 split as a 45% crash and liquidated about 400 traders ([RedStone](https://www.redstone.finance/research/tokenized-stocks-2026)). xStocks are also tracker certificates issued by a Jersey entity, with a $100K minimum for primary minting and no shareholder rights ([Allium](https://www.allium.so/blog/xstocks-tokenized-stocks-you-dont-legally-own/)).

For Mercury, lending its own dollar from a pool against xStocks would copy how JupUSD is lent on Jupiter Lend. Depositors bear the losses, and deposits cap capacity. Demand is the binding constraint: Kamino's xStocks market carried only **$5.3M of debt** in September 2026 ([Kamino](https://gov.kamino.finance/t/kamino-lend-monthly-risk-insights-september-2026/893)).

Owning the dollar also adds a dependency that USDC lending lacks. A liquidator must turn seized xStocks into Mercury dollars. If only vetted market makers can mint at par, the lending market's solvency comes to depend on the depth of the Mercury-dollar/USDC market, or on a Mercury backstop that redeems at par.

### Minting against xStocks would need a second token

Minting a dollar against stocks, the way a collateralised-debt-position (CDP) protocol does, has real attractions. A debt ceiling, not depositor supply, sets capacity; the stability fee (the interest rate CDP borrowers pay) becomes a policy lever; interest accrues to the issuer or its savers; and minted dollars that stay in the app never touch USDC. But a white-label issuer mints only 1:1 against deposits. In a Bridge- or Paxos-issued coin, no one else can mint, so xStock collateral cannot create supply. A CDP therefore needs a Mercury-controlled token with several issuance channels.

GHO shows the pattern. Each GHO "facilitator" has its own capacity cap: the Aave lending market mints against collateral, while swap modules exchange GHO 1:1 for USDC or USDT ([Aave GHO docs](https://aave.com/docs/primitives/gho)). The Mercury version would pair a swap module holding the regulated dollar with a small, separately capped channel minting against xStocks. That token would not be a regulated payment stablecoin, and ESMA's September 2026 review response specifically targets service providers that handle unauthorised stablecoins ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/ESMA75-113276571-1721_Response_to_the_EC_consultation_MiCA_regulation_review.pdf)).

GHO also shows how a CDP dollar breaks. In August 2026 it was the cheapest stablecoin to borrow on Aave, so borrowers minted it and sold it. The USDC swap module ended up "effectively empty", the USDT module fell from 53.2M to 40.8M in two weeks, and Aave's stewards had to raise rates ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/gho-stewards-august-2026-gho-borrow-rate-and-aave-savings-rate-update/25534)). In 2023, GHO had launched without any swap module and traded near $0.965 even after rate hikes to 5.22% ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-increase-gho-borrow-rate-to-5-22-on-aave-v3/15632)).

That sets the conditions for a Mercury CDP. Its stability fee cannot undercut market USDC borrow rates, which run at 6.25% in Kamino's xStocks market ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/yields/pool/571311bf-7da7-4f3c-b181-43119070bffc)); its swap module must be sized to the CDP book; and someone needs bounded authority to move rates quickly, as GHO's stewards can by up to 500 bps every two days, capped at 25%. Bad debt from a Monday-open price gap would hit the dollar's own backing, not third-party lenders. Precedent reserves of 0.8–1.3% of supply are small against equity gap risk, so a reserve should be sized to stress losses on the CDP book itself. The economics are modest: a $25M book at 4.5–6% earns $1.1–1.5M a year before provisioning. Liquidation penalties are not a reliable revenue line; Sky collected only about $3.9M a year from them on billions of debt ([Block Analitica](https://blockanalitica.substack.com/p/exploring-the-sky-ecosystem-skys-3bd)).

Solana's history argues against hurrying. Hubble's USDH shrank to **$2.7M against a $3.8B Solana stablecoin market** ([Step Data](https://stepdata.substack.com/p/step-data-insights-solana-stablecoins)). UXD shut down at $7.5M of deposits, saying its model was "not exciting enough for DeFi users" ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/solana-stablecoin-uxd-protocol-with-75m-usd-tvl-shuts-down/)). Hylo's no-liquidation design kept its hyUSD at par through a 69% SOL drawdown, but only because its junior token was diluted 28x and fell about 99% ([DefiLlama Research](https://defillama.com/research/spotlight/hylo-xsol-no-liquidation-no-funding-but-what-are-the-real-costs)); the losses moved rather than disappeared.

## Committed makers, not incentives, give a new dollar its depth

The JupUSD gap shows that routing coverage matters as much as raw depth: an app quoting only through Jupiter's own router would misprice large redemptions of its own dollar by a third.

Token-2022 did not stop USDG or PYUSD getting tight markets, despite their permanent delegates. Its cost shows up instead in DeFi integrations. One DeFi-safety helper library rejects mints with confidential transfers, permanent delegates or hooks outright ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/hopper-token-2022)), and Jupiter shows warnings on permanent-delegate tokens ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/solana-token-extensions-payments)). PYUSD and USDG overcame this through partnerships, and a small brand would have to do the same. These quotes were taken once, without a wallet attached to execute them, so weekend and stress-period depth is untested.

Paid incentives rent liquidity rather than build it. Paxos's PYUSD push in 2024 reportedly cost about **$500k a week** (who paid is unconfirmed), and Kamino's PYUSD reserve peaked near $350M at about 18% before caps and yields were cut ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/kamino-seeing-benefits-from-liquidity-incentives-on-pyusd); [Blockworks](https://www.blockworks.com/news/lightspeed-newsletter-pyusd-outflows-solana)). PYUSD is now sustained by curated vaults and real borrowing. In September 2026, Kamino held $217.5M and $131.6M in two PYUSD vaults ([Kamino](https://gov.kamino.finance/t/kamino-lend-monthly-risk-insights-september-2026/893)), and Jupiter Lend's PYUSD reserve grew from $50.1M to $252M in two weeks, 96% of it borrowed ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)). The Plasma chain shows the same pattern at larger scale: its stablecoins fell from about $6.35B to $1.28B once its rewards lost value ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/chain/plasma)).

### Makers want redemption at par and order flow, not rebates

JupUSD shows the core tool for holding the peg. Direct minting and redemption is limited to "registered benefactors (KYC'd/KYB'd market makers and institutional partners)". These makers arbitrage the peg alongside an automated bot, mint and redemption cost a flat **0.04%**, and retail users buy on exchanges ([Jupiter docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/jupusd/index)). Among the coins checked, Solana has no Maker-style swap module; the issuers' own mint and redemption programs fill that role.

Rebates are too small to buy quoting. Hyperliquid's top maker rebate is 0.3 bp ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/Hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)). The Solana Foundation's Frontier programme pays 0.1–0.5 bp across 16 venues, and entry requires $10M of 30-day volume ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-frontier-trader-rewards-program/)). The token-loan-plus-call-option deals used to launch new tokens make no sense for a $1 asset, and they carry reputational risk: in the Movement case, a maker sold about 66M MOVE, roughly $38M, on listing day ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/317524/movement-labs-suspends-co-founder-amid-market-maker-controversy)).

What makers do accept is measurable obligations. Flowdesk suggests judging spread and depth together, with uptime above 95% ([Flowdesk](https://flowdesk.co/updates/blogs/67215e228e4bf46d9bd3f247)). JupiterZ, Jupiter's request-for-quote (RFQ) system, switches off makers who fill less than 95% of their quotes in an hour or miss a 250 ms response window ([Jupiter dev docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap/routing/rfq-integration)).

A Mercury maker package would therefore centre on two to four vetted makers who can mint and redeem at par, at or below JupUSD's 0.04%, with same-day or 24/7 settlement. Around them would sit a capped on-chain redemption buffer, seeded stable pools on Orca or Meteora or a slot on Perena's hub-and-spoke exchange (which lends depth from a USDC/USDT/PYUSD core pool to newer stablecoins) ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/projects/perena)), quoting on JupiterZ and the Manifest order book, and listings on Kamino and Jupiter Lend so makers can finance their inventory. The terms should be public, as Jupiter discloses its own market-maker deals ([Jupiter docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/more/jup-token/transparency)). Venue share shifts fast. Proprietary AMMs, each run by a single market maker that updates prices from its own oracle, carry over 60% of Solana DEX volume ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/learn/Lightspeed/solanas-next-big-catalyst-in-2026-lightspeed-weekly-roundup)). One of them, HumidiFi, fell from 68% to 26.5% of that segment in a single month ([Stepdata](https://stepdata.substack.com/p/prop-amms-on-solana-bisonfi-ends)). Mercury's relationships with makers will matter more than its relationships with any one venue.

Kraken says the price mismatch at Monday's open has historically stayed under 1% ([Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/articles/xstocks-faq)).

Mercury cannot easily recruit xStock makers of its own, because hedging needs a broker relationship and Backed onboarding. It should plug into the makers already quoting.

That also settles how the dollar meets the stocks. Makers quote xStocks against USDC, so the Mercury dollar should convert to USDC at par through Mercury's own mint and redemption at the moment a user trades. Mercury does not need pools pairing its dollar with xStocks.

For lending against xStocks, Jupiter Lend's sizing rule is the one to copy. Its liquidity is "entirely via RFQ", with more than $100M of sell-side depth per asset. Each asset carries a $10M borrow cap, under a policy that reads: "if available sell-side liquidity falls below $30M for any asset, borrow cap should be reduced correspondingly" ([Jupiter Lend risk report](https://static-r2.jup.ag/lend/risk-research/xstocks-risk-report.pdf)).

How Mercury routes user orders is a regulatory choice as much as a pricing one. The least intermediated option is to keep routing through Jupiter's Ultra engine with a disclosed app fee, as Phantom does at 0.85% ([Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/cryptocurrency/wallets/phantom-wallet-review)) on top of Jupiter's own 0–0.5% ([Jupiter docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/trade/spot/fees)). A middle option is a Mercury quote auction among whitelisted makers, with the Ultra price as the floor for best execution. The most intermediated is netting users' opposing orders internally, which gives the best prices but makes Mercury a matching venue with broker-like duties. Taking payments from makers for routing user flow would resemble payment for order flow, which MiFIR Article 39a bars EU investment firms from receiving. Ultra users were sandwiched at only 0.7 times the rate chance predicts, against 11–19 times for trading bots ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)). But RFQ volume is easy to inflate; one day alone saw $2.42B of round-trip trades (same source). Any maker rewards should be tied to price improvement for users, not to volume.

### User liquidity should stay on the stable side

Products that put retail money to work as liquidity fall into three risk tiers. The lowest-risk tier is a Mercury-dollar/USDC stable pool or a lending deposit: impermanent loss is near zero while both coins hold par, returns come from swap fees and any yield share, and the main risks are a depeg and the admin key. The middle tier is automated pool vaults pairing xStocks with dollars, which expose users to losses that grow with price variance ([Milionis et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06046v2)) and to weekend gaps, and which get picked off by oracle-driven makers who reprice faster than the pool. The riskiest tier is vaults where users act as the house against traders. Hyperliquid's HLP vault carried about $12M of unrealised loss in the JELLY squeeze until validators delisted the market and settled it at a different price ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348314/hyperliquid-delists-jellyjelly-memecoin-amid-whale-manipulation-fiasco)). It later absorbed about $4.9M of bad debt from a POPCAT manipulation and paused deposits and withdrawals ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/large-popcat-trades-result-in-usd5-million-loss-for-hyperliquid-vault)). Jupiter's JLP rose 38% in Q3 2026 while SOL rose 59%. It saw about $109M of net redemptions and earned $12M from trader losses, down from $23M in Q2 ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jupiter-quarterly-report-q3-2026)).

The largest real-world loss vector across all three tiers is admin control, not the strategy. Drift lost about $285M after a 2-of-5 multisig with no timelock was used to list fake collateral ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/drift-protocol-incident-multisig-governance-compromise-via-durable-nonce-exploitation)). Any user-liquidity product Mercury offers should show returns net of these losses, publish a stress history, and sit behind timelocked admin keys.

## Privacy in 2026 hides amounts by default and parties only behind caps

### Confidential transfers work, but the issuer holds the switch

Solana's confidential transfers are a Token-2022 feature that encrypts balances and transfer amounts. They were switched off on 19 June 2025 after a flaw that allowed proofs to be forged, and re-enabled on 4 June 2026. While they were off, confidential balances could not move ([xroot.dev](https://xroot.dev/blog/solana-confidential-transfers-kill-switch-proof-cost); [Solana post-mortem](https://solana.com/news/post-mortem-june-25-2025)). The v1 transaction format went live on 15 September 2026 and raised the size limit to 4,096 bytes, enough for a full set of proofs in one transaction ([xroot.dev](https://xroot.dev/blog/solana-confidential-transfers-kill-switch-proof-cost)).

What the feature hides is narrow: "Token account addresses remain public. Only amounts and balances are private." Moving funds into or out of a confidential balance shows the amount in cleartext ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer)).

The costs are manageable for consumer payments. Range proofs cost 111k–368k compute units, "roughly two orders of magnitude" more than a plain transfer; each enabled account needs about 0.0015 SOL of extra rent; and decrypting a balance takes about a second on a phone ([xroot.dev](https://xroot.dev/blog/solana-confidential-transfers-kill-switch-proof-cost); [Solana integration guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/integration-guide)).

A mint can hold one auditor key, which receives every transfer amount encrypted to it. That key cannot spend funds or see full balances, and rotating it covers only future transfers. With the standard key setup, one wallet uses one encryption key pair across all mints, so sharing a user's key discloses all of that user's confidential accounts ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer)).

The Token-2022 source code shows what still works under confidentiality. Transfer hooks still run, receiving `u64::MAX` in place of the hidden amount, so a know-your-customer allowlist or sanctions check keeps working because the hook still sees the parties. Freeze and pause are enforced. The permanent delegate, however, is never consulted ([token-2022 source](https://github.com/solana-program/token-2022/blob/main/program/src/extension/confidential_transfer/processor.rs)). By inference from the code, an issuer's clawback power therefore does not reach confidential balances. That should be confirmed with Token-2022 maintainers before anyone relies on it for seizure policy. It might also help explain the adoption numbers: of **495,503** PYUSD token accounts, only two had configured confidential balances, both unapproved; USDG had none of 190,581, and TSLAx none of 145,474 ([Solscan: PYUSD](https://solscan.io/token/2b1kV6DkPAnxd5ixfnxCpjxmKwqjjaYmCZfHsFu24GXo)). That explanation is a hypothesis, not a finding.

On a rented mint, the issuer holds the confidential-transfer authority and approves every account, so private balances in Mercury are a negotiation before they are a feature. If Mercury controls a token layer, a workable configuration would pair manual approval tied to know-your-customer checks with a Mercury auditor key held in a hardware security module and a logged rotation history, a transfer-hook allowlist, pause and freeze, and an open public deposit-and-withdrawal path so users can still exit if the proof program is ever switched off again. The wallet side is new work. The standard key derivation needs a dedicated signing flow for the `solana-conf-bal/v1` message. Proofs must be built wherever the encryption secret lives, and building them on Mercury's servers would mean Mercury holds every user's view key ([Solana integration guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/integration-guide)). No source shows whether Privy supports this derivation.

Mercury's relayer can pay the rent for proof accounts. But the v1 transaction format "does not support address lookup tables" ([Solana](https://solana.com/upgrades/larger-transaction-sizes)), so fitting a confidential transfer and a Jupiter swap into one transaction may be tight.

The ordering trades custody against maturity. The omnibus ledger is the easiest to build and gives the best user experience. But it makes Mercury the custodian and the single point of disclosure, reduces Privy self-custody to a formality for those funds, and sends external payments out of Mercury's hot wallet. Partner pools keep custody with users but come with deposit caps and young code. Elusiv, Solana's earlier privacy protocol, shut down in February 2024 after processing over $125M ([QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/solana-development/legacy/privacy-with-elusiv)). Solflare's Private Send is the lightest version: an opt-in route through Houdini Swap, chosen per transaction and off by default ([SpendNode](https://www.spendnode.io/blog/solflare-privacy-aggregator-layer-pal-private-send-solana-houdini-swap/)).

### Investing privacy collides with borrowing and self-custody

Backed's xStocks already carry an initialised confidential-transfer configuration, with manual approval and no auditor. Ondo's Solana stock tokens carry the same, without a permanent delegate ([Solscan: TSLAx](https://solscan.io/token/XsDoVfqeBukxuZHWhdvWHBhgEHjGNst4MLodqsJHzoB); [Solana Foundation](https://solana.com/news/ondo-global-markets-tokenized-stocks-etfs-solana)). Even if Backed approved Mercury's users, confidential balances would still show which stock a user holds, because every token account names its mint, and buys from exchanges would still arrive publicly. Worse, confidential balances cannot be used by on-chain programs, the gap Arcium's C-SPL standard is meant to close ([Arcium](https://www.arcium.com/articles/arcium-roadmap-update)), so private holdings would block the very DeFi borrowing Mercury offers against them.

The realistic route to "nobody can see what I hold" is an omnibus vault. Mercury would hold xStocks in a few pooled accounts, keep per-user sub-ledgers, and execute netted orders in batches through RFQ. That makes Mercury a custodian and a broker-like intermediary. Borrowing against those positions would become an internal, custodial credit book, the structure behind the SEC's $100M BlockFi and $45M Nexo settlements ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2022-26); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/203983/nexo-pays-45-million-to-settle-sec-and-state-charges-for-failing-to-register-lending-product)).

The baseline every design should adopt protects users from front-running rather than from observation. Options include Jito's encrypted block-building marketplace, which keeps transactions "confidential until execution" inside secure enclaves ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/block-assembly-marketplace-bam)), Jito's DontFront flag ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/docs/defi/mev-protection)), and Helius MEV Protect ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/mev-protect)), combined with limit or RFQ execution.

### Compliance tolerates accountable privacy, not anonymity

The EU line is explicit. From **10 July 2027**, Article 79 of the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation bars crypto-asset service providers from keeping "any account otherwise allowing for the anonymisation of the customer account holder or the anonymisation or increased obfuscation of transactions" ([Springlex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/aml/amlr-regulation/article-79/)). Self-hosted wallets are not banned, while MiCA Article 76(3) restricts trading of tokens with built-in anonymisation unless holders can be identified ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/amlr-crypto-casp-requirements/)).

The travel rule, which requires sender and recipient details to accompany a transfer, also bites. Under the EU rules, transfers above €1,000 to or from a self-hosted wallet need proof that the customer owns it, and records must be kept for five years ([General Bytes](https://generalbytes.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ESD/pages/3779526658/EU+Specific+TR+Details+and+Instructions)). The FATF's revised standard expects countries to be ready by end-2030 ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/R16-Public-Consultation-June-2026.html)). Because confidential transfers hide amounts, Mercury would have to send travel-rule details off-chain, using amounts decrypted with its auditor key.

Regulated precedents all point to privacy with accountable disclosure. Paxos's USAD, backed by USDG reserves, has run on Aleo with confidential identities and amounts since 11 February 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260211924754/en/Paxos-Labs-Aleo-Network-Foundation-Announce-USAD-Stablecoin-Live-on-Aleo-Mainnet)); Zama's confidential USDT wrapper held $7.7M of shielded value by mid-September 2026 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/usdt-live-zama-confidential-transactions/)); and Circle lists confidential balances with view keys on its Arc chain as "in development" ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-launches-arc-mainnet-an-economic-operating-system-for-the-internet)). Demand for screened privacy is modest: Privacy Pools held about $9M after 18 months on Ethereum ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/privacy-pools)). The US signal remains cautionary. Tornado Cash developer Roman Storm's conviction for conspiracy to run an unlicensed money-transmitting business still stands, pending a motion to acquit, with a retrial on the other counts set for April 2027 ([Crypto Valley Journal](https://cryptovalleyjournal.com/focus/legal-and-compliance/tornado-cash-roman-storm-trial-delayed-to-april-2027/)).

The posture that fits a KYC'd app combines screening at entry and exit, auditor and view keys held by Mercury, the ability to freeze or refuse transfers, geo-blocking, an always-open public exit and documented procedures for lawful access. That is privacy from the public chain, not from Mercury. Whether "private from the public but visible to the service provider" counts as "increased obfuscation" under Article 79 is unresolved, and counsel needs to answer it before July 2027 for any EU user.

## Order the steps by what each one proves

The evidence suggests an order in which each step tests the premise of the next. It also exposes a few decisions that cannot be undone.

The single number that decides most of what follows is how much dollar float originated by Mercury sits idle in users' wallets. Mercury can measure it before branding anything: distribute an open-loop dollar such as OUSD or USDG, or negotiate USDC partner terms, while keeping cash spendable through conversion at par. On Bridge's quoted reserve mixes, $50M of idle float earns about $1.1–1.65M a year gross and $250M about $5.5–8.3M, before issuer fees.

A branded coin becomes worth it once three conditions hold. The float must cover the issuance fee and the cost of building liquidity from zero. The issuer must agree authority and exit terms, at minimum a Mercury auditor key on confidential transfers, a hook program Mercury specifies, and written exit terms covering notice periods, mint-authority handover and reserve wind-down, none of which any provider publishes. And the issuing entity's jurisdiction should suit non-US users and avoid the US related-third-party presumption. Three candidates stand out: Bridge, for stack alignment with Privy and the CASH precedent; Paxos, for its MAS and MiCA issuing entities and a mint setup that already has tight depth; and Coinbase Custom, whose USDC backing and 1:1 USDC redemption simplify exits.

The configuration chosen at mint creation is the irreversible decision. Token-2022 with confidential transfers initialised, a hook slot and a pause switch keeps privacy and compliance options open, at some cost in DeFi integrations. A classic SPL mint, as JupUSD chose, closes those options for good.

Earn and lending come next, as a single product. Users would hold an opt-in wrapper share token whose Mercury dollars are lent to xStock borrowers in an isolated market, starting with SPYx and QQQx. Caps would be tied to measured RFQ depth, and disclosure would say "variable rate" rather than "interest" or "savings". The gating fact is borrower demand. Kamino's xStocks market carries only about $5M of debt today, so products that create borrowing, such as spending against stocks, are really earn products.

How much reserve income can flow into the wrapper depends on the issuer's jurisdiction and on rules not yet final. The Fed's comment period closes 30 November 2026, and GENIUS takes effect by 18 January 2027 at the latest. EU users may need a different product, or none, given the EBA and ESCB push to extend the interest ban to lending.

Privacy runs on a parallel track that inherits the mint decision. It starts with confidential transfers initialised but public by default, then opt-in private balances for KYC'd users once the Privy key flow and auditor indexing exist, then unlinkable peer-to-peer payments through a screened partner pool behind caps. Investing privacy comes last, and only through an omnibus model if Mercury accepts custodian licensing; without that, the honest offer is front-running protection plus RFQ execution. The CDP and an in-house issuer belong at the end of the sequence, as the table of options and gates shows.

The strongest case against branding at all deserves weight. Circle, OUSD and USDG already pay distributors most of the economics. USDH showed an incumbent dollar beating a new one even on the new coin's home venue. And a Mercury coin adds three risks: dependence on one issuer, the cost of building exit liquidity, and look-alike tokens squatting on its ticker. The case for branding is control of a token layer, the brand itself, earning on supply that leaves the app, and a path toward eventual self-issuance. On this evidence, branding should follow measured float and negotiated terms, not precede them.

## Conclusion

"Owning the dollar" splits into four separable assets: the brand, the reserve economics, the on-chain token authorities and the issuer licence. In 2026 only the first two are cheap, and distribution programs have made the second nearly free without any brand. What renting cannot supply is the token layer: the auditor key, the hook program, and the right to add new channels that mint supply. Those are exactly what amount-private payments and a stock-backed minting channel require. So Mercury's real strategic question is not which issuer to pick. It is who will hold the confidential-transfer authority and the hook program, and that must be written into the contract before the mint is created, because the mint's configuration cannot change afterwards.

Mercury's revenue and its earn product also pull in opposite directions. The dollar earns most for Mercury when users hold it idle to pay, send and spend. That makes payments, cards and privacy features that keep balances in the app the real revenue engine, while earn is a retention tool and a source of borrowing demand, priced near Treasury yields. And the privacy most worth marketing to investors, hidden holdings, can only be built today by becoming the kind of custodial lender regulators have already fined. Until confidential balances become usable by on-chain programs, a self-custodial Mercury can offer privacy of amounts, not of positions.
