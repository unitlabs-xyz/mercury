# Where USD-cash (USDC) yield comes from in crypto, and what a Solana consumer app can tap or replicate

Data conventions used throughout:

- "Snapshot 2026-10-21" = pulled live from the DefiLlama yields API (`yields.llama.fi/pools`, `/lendBorrow`, `/chart/{pool}`) on 2026-10-21. Pool pages are cited as `defillama.com/yields/pool/{id}`. DefiLlama "TVL" for lending pools and lending protocols means supplied minus borrowed (free liquidity), not deposits; deposit/borrow sizes below come from the `/lendBorrow` endpoint.
- "Yearly avg" = simple mean of DefiLlama daily supply-APY points for that calendar year (2026 = Jan 1 to Oct 21). Computed by me from DefiLlama chart data.
- APY figures from DefiLlama include reward-token incentives where stated ("base" excludes incentives).
- Short-rate context: The FOMC held the fed funds target at 3.50–3.75% on June 17, 2026 ([Advisor Perspectives](https://advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/06/18/feds-interest-rate-decision-june-17-2026)). ## Q1. Over-collateralized lending: what USDC earns on Solana (Kamino, Jupiter Lend, marginfi/Project 0, Save, Drift) and EVM (Aave, Morpho, Compound, Euler) in 2024–2026; what drives utilization; how spreads and reserve factors work

### Takeaway

Supplying USDC to over-collateralized lending markets paid about 7–11% on average in 2024 (a leverage-driven bull market), about 4–5% in 2025, and about 3.4–4.5% so far in 2026. Rates spike briefly whenever utilization passes the kink. Supply APY equals borrow APR × utilization × (1 − reserve factor/take rate). Take rates run about 10% (Aave, Jupiter Lend) and range 0–35% on Kamino. Solana's lending venues shrank and concentrated a lot in 2026 after the Drift exploit on April 1 and the cross-market rsETH/KelpDAO shock on April 18. Kamino and Jupiter Lend now dominate.

**Mechanics: rate curves, reserve factors, utilization drivers**

- Aave v3 Ethereum Core USDC rate model in April 2026: base 0%, slope1 about 3.5%, slope2 about 10%, optimal utilization 92%, reserve factor 10%. On April 22, 2026, after the April 18 rsETH/KelpDAO event, utilization reached 99.87% with under $3M available liquidity, 13.82% borrow and 12.42% supply. The post estimates about $300M of extra borrowing within 72 hours "driven largely by users seeking dollar liquidity from positions whose normal exit paths were blocked." The proposal to raise slope2 to 40–50% and cut the kink to 85–87% met opposition and was effectively dropped (thread closed May 25, 2026) — [Aave governance ARFC](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-improve-liquidity-buffer-for-usdc-on-aave-v3-ethereum-core-raise-slope-2-lower-optimal-utilization/24684)
- Coin Metrics reportedly found daily midnight-UTC USDC utilization spikes on Aave, caused by about $190M being pulled and redeposited within an hour (apparently for balance attestations), which costs USDC borrowers about $6M/yr — [KuCoin flash summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/aave-whale-midnight-siphoning-costs-borrowers-6m-annually); [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/why-is-aaves-usdc-utilization-rate-spiking) (secondary, undated)
- Jupiter Lend: a 10% reserve factor applies to all borrow interest. "Borrowers pay the full interest rate, lenders receive 90%, and 10% goes to the protocol treasury." Earn charges no deposit fees — [Jupiter Lend FAQ](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/lend/faq.md); [Jupiter developer docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/lend/earn.md)

Rates follow a piecewise-linear utilization curve — [Blockworks Kamino financials](https://blockworks.com/analytics/kamino/kamino-financials); [klend docs (mintlify mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/kamino-finance/klend/concepts/reserves)

- Borrow-demand drivers visible in the data: leveraged LST/JLP loops (the Kamino JLP Market USDC sits at 99% utilization and 10.24% borrow), RWA and private-credit collateral markets (OnRe, Figure, Huma, Maple), and stress-driven liquidity demand (the Aave April 2026 event) — [DefiLlama lendBorrow API](https://yields.llama.fi/lendBorrow); [Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-improve-liquidity-buffer-for-usdc-on-aave-v3-ethereum-core-raise-slope-2-lower-optimal-utilization/24684)
- Kamino launched xStocks collateral on July 14, 2025, using Chainlink price feeds, in isolated markets kept separate from core pools. ### Inferences

- In 2025–2026, USDC lending on both Solana and EVM has mostly paid in the 3.5–6% band, roughly 0–2 pp above T-bills. The 2024 averages of 7–11% reflected leverage demand in a bull market and are not a steady-state baseline.
- Utilization near or above the kink (90–99%) is normal for the large USDC reserves. Depositors therefore face real liquidity risk: withdrawals can be blocked for hours or days in stress, as on Aave in April 2026.
- Borrowers in the Kamino xStocks market today pay about 6.25%, not 7–8%. The market is also very small ($5.7M USDC). A Mercury-native market would need its own borrower base and would compete with that rate.
- The Drift collapse shows that a protocol's admin-key and governance security matters as much as the lending math. A Mercury protocol would carry this risk directly.

### Gaps

- No DefiLlama series covers marginfi/Project 0 or Drift USDC lending rates for 2024–2025. Their historical rates are missing.
- I could not confirm whether the Kamino "SOL/BTC Market" USDC pool is the renamed former "Main Market". Its 2023 start date suggests so, but that is unverified.
- No primary source gave Kamino's exact reserve parameters (take rate, kink, slopes) for the xStocks market USDC reserve. The roughly 10% is implied.
- The `chartLendBorrow` endpoint returned errors, so I have no borrow-rate or utilization history (only current borrow rates).

## Q2. Tokenized T-bills and money-market funds: BUIDL, USDY/OUSG, USYC, USTB, BENJI, USDM and others. Which are on Solana, who may hold them, redemption, fees, yields

### Takeaway

Tokenized T-bill and MMF products yield about 3.5–3.9% (October 2026), roughly the policy rate minus fees. Several are live on Solana: BUIDL (about $886M on Solana), USDY (about $180M), USYC, USTB and BENJI. But all except USDY are restricted to qualified purchasers, non-US institutions, or other allowlisted investors. USDY is open to non-US retail, after KYC and a lock-up. A US-facing consumer app cannot pass these directly to US retail users. They mainly suit treasury management or a non-US product.

### Inferences

- Gross yield is anchored to T-bills (about 3.6–3.9% now). Net to users depends on the fund fee (often 0.15–0.50%, not verified here) and on any app fee.
- For a US consumer app, BUIDL, USYC, USTB and OUSG are not usable as retail pass-through because of QP, institutional or allowlist gating. USDY is excluded for US persons. Mercury could still hold such tokens as an entity (subject to eligibility), for example to earn on the idle part of a lending pool. Passing that yield to users raises securities and investment-company questions (see Q7).
- Several Kamino markets already accept tokenized T-bill and RWA collateral (Superstate, OnRe, Figure, Maple). This creates USDC borrow demand from RWA loopers, a second borrower base beyond xStocks.

### Gaps

- Fund-level management fees for BUIDL, USYC, USTB, BENJI and OUSG were not verified.

## Q3. Stablecoin issuer reward programs that share reserve income with distributors (Paxos USDG/GDN, PayPal PYUSD, Coinbase USDC, Agora AUSD): partner terms and Solana support

### Takeaway

Issuer revenue-share programs pay the distributor (the app), not the end holder. USDG's Global Dollar Network is the clearest open program. It shares up to about 97–100% of reserve income, by secondary accounts, based on partners' held, minted or accepted balances, and it works on Solana. PYUSD's roughly 4% reward applies only to balances inside PayPal and Venmo. Coinbase's USDC reward is Coinbase-platform-only and since December 2025 limited to Coinbase One members. These programs fund app-level "rewards". Their legality for retail pass-through in the US is being narrowed by the OCC and CLARITY Act activity (Q7).

### Cited Findings

- **USDG / Global Dollar Network (Paxos)**: USDG launched on Solana on Feb 25, 2025 with five partners (alfred, Caliza, Noah, Rain, Sling Money) — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/353947/global-dollar-network-new-members-solana-usdg-stablecoin). GDN later grew to 100+ partners, and USDG passed a $1B market cap — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=256468). Alpaca joined in October 2025 to bring USDG to customers on Solana — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251029084657/en/Alpaca-Joins-Global-Dollar-Network-Bringing-USDG-to-Customers-on-Solana)
- USDG partner terms as described by a partner (Fordefi): USDG held in enrolled vaults earns rewards with no lock-ups, and the USDG never leaves the vault. Paxos measures eligible balances. The APY "is set by the Global Dollar Network and changes over time." Rewards apply on Ethereum, Solana, Robinhood Chain, X Layer and Ink. EU-incorporated organizations are not eligible "under MiCA." With a single-pool design, depositor APR = borrow APR × utilization × (1 − protocol fee). At 7–8% borrow and 70–80% utilization with a 10% fee, depositors get about 4.4–5.8% APR and Mercury earns about $0.49–0.64M a year per $100M of deposits. Today the Kamino xStocks market has $5.2M borrowed at 6.25%. Close precedents exist: Coinbase lends users' USDC through Morpho on Base and shares a 25% curator fee, Robinhood Earn routes USDG into Morpho/Spark, and brokerages fund margin loans with customer cash.

## Q7. Regulatory constraints on passing yield to retail: GENIUS Act, SEC treatment of yield products, EU MiCA

### Takeaway

The GENIUS Act (signed July 18, 2025) bars stablecoin issuers from paying holders interest or yield "solely in connection with" holding the coin. In February 2026 the OCC proposed extending that to affiliate and related-party arrangements. The Senate's CLARITY Act compromise (May 1, 2026) would ban deposit-like passive yield by intermediaries while allowing activity-based rewards, including for "providing liquidity in DeFi protocols." I found no confirmation that the CLARITY Act has been enacted as of October 2026. MiCA Articles 40 and 50 ban interest on ARTs and EMTs by both issuers and CASPs. In the US, the SEC has historically treated custodial pooled crypto-lending yield products as securities (BlockFi, Nexo, Coinbase Lend).

### Cited Findings

- GENIUS Act §4(a)(11): no permitted or foreign payment stablecoin issuer "shall pay the holder of any payment stablecoin any form of interest or yield (whether in cash, tokens, or other consideration) solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention of such payment stablecoin" — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/passle/2025/08/navigating-the-genius-acts-framework-for-digital-asset-regulation); [Mondaq (trade groups on loopholes)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/financial-services/1667174/trade-groups-urge-congress-to-address-genius-act-loopholes). It was signed into law on July 18, 2025. It applies to the issuer, leaving an "affiliate loophole" for exchanges — [Upshift](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/how-the-genius-act-and-clarity-act-reshape-stablecoin-yield)
- OCC NPRM (Feb 25, 2026): reads the yield ban broadly, covering OCC-supervised institutions and their affiliates, and would treat issuer-funded yield through affiliates or related third parties as inconsistent with the Act. The White House Council of Economic Advisers (April 8, 2026) concluded that a passive-yield ban could meaningfully affect consumers and might do little for bank deposit stability — [Sheppard Mullin via JD Supra, Apr 21, 2026](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/stablecoin-yield-saga-continues-occ-3886986/)
- CLARITY Act Senate compromise (reached May 1, 2026): bans payments "economically or functionally equivalent to the payment of interest or yield on an interest-bearing bank deposit," permits rewards tied to bona fide activities (transactions, payments, transfers, remittances, providing liquidity in DeFi protocols), and gives regulators one year to write clarifying rules. The Senate text still had to be reconciled with the House version — [DL News, May 4, 2026](https://dlnews.com/articles/regulation/clarity-act-odds-surge-on-stablecoin-compromise/); [Baker McKenzie, May 5, 2026](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2026/05/05/the-clarity-acts-yield-compromise-what-the-senate-actually-agreed-to-and-why-it-matters/). Earlier, the markup was delayed over the yield dispute — [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/us-senator-pushes-to-move-clarity-act-markup-to-may-amid-stablecoin-yield-dispute)
- SEC history: BlockFi interest accounts were securities, and BlockFi operated as an unregistered investment company ($100M settlement, Feb 14, 2022) — [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2022-26). ### Inferences

- Lending yield (borrowers pay interest) is a different legal category from issuer-paid reserve yield. The GENIUS ban does not reach it by its terms, but the SEC's custodial-lending cases are the relevant precedent. Non-custodial, user-directed lending into a protocol (the Coinbase/Robinhood model) is the structure large US platforms have chosen in 2025–2026.
- Distributor revenue-share from issuers (GDN, Circle) to an app is still legal to receive. Passing it to end users as passive "interest" is what the OCC rule and the CLARITY text target. Activity-linked rewards, including DeFi liquidity provision, are the carve-out under discussion.
- EU users are effectively excluded from any interest-like feature on USDC/USDG held via a CASP.
