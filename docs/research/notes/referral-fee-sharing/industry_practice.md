# Referral and affiliate programs at exchanges, brokers and tokenized-stock platforms (as of October 2026): terms, enforcement, and lower-risk structures

Scope note: research run 21 October 2026. Program terms change often, so each finding carries the date of the terms where one was available. Non-US focus; US items are included only for comparison. This is not legal advice. Where a figure comes only from an affiliate/referral-code site or a search snippet rather than the platform's own page, it is marked "(secondary)".

Mercury's proposal, for comparison throughout: the inviter gets 25–35% of Mercury's 0.5% fee on the friend's trades for 12 months, paid monthly in USDC, with a 7-day hold and clawbacks for quick round trips.

## 1. Crypto exchanges' fee-share referral and affiliate programs (Binance, Bybit, OKX, Kraken, Coinbase, Bitget, KuCoin): rates, exclusions, country limits, KYC, caps, and affiliate vs user referral

### Takeaway

Paying a share of fees is standard at offshore or "global" crypto exchanges. User-referral rates run about 20–30% for 12 months or open-ended, and approved affiliates get up to 50–60%. The programs come with clear guardrails: KYC on the referrer, commission only on net fees actually paid (zero-fee pairs, coupons and VIP/market-maker flow excluded), quarterly or monthly caps, risk-review holds, bans on self-referral, wash trading and multiple accounts, and region-specific entities. None of the official terms reviewed lists tokenized stocks or xStocks as commission-eligible. Kraken explicitly excludes "equities," and Bybit's own stock products are fee-free or not named. Under MiCA and in the UK, the same brands switch to different, usually flat or coupon-based, programs.

- User referral is self-serve for any verified user, at lower rates (Bybit 20–30%) or flat bonuses (Kraken Pro $20). — [Bybit](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Bybit-Referral-Program-General-FAQ); [Kraken Pro](https://support.kraken.com/articles/kraken-pro-app-referrals)

### Inferences

- Mercury's 25–35% for 12 months sits squarely inside the crypto-exchange user-referral range: Bybit pays 20–30% for 365 days, OKX 30% open-ended, Coinbase 50% for 3 months. On rate and duration alone it is not an outlier. What differs is the product. The large exchanges' fee-share terms are written around crypto spot and derivatives, and the reviewed terms either exclude equities (Kraken), don't list stock tokens, or apply where the stock product is fee-free (Bybit Stocks, Kraken USDG buys). No major exchange was found advertising fee-share referral commissions specifically on tokenized-stock trades.
- The guardrails the exchanges converge on are KYC on the referrer, a net-fee base, exclusion of zero-fee or discounted flow, caps, a short risk-review hold, an inactivity cutoff, and region carve-outs. Mercury's 7-day hold and round-trip clawback match this pattern. A per-referrer cap and a referrer-KYC requirement are the most visible missing pieces in Mercury's plan compared with these terms.
- Exchanges with EU/UK licences (Bybit EU, Coinbase under MiFID, Kraken's "where permitted" clause) run different, usually flat or coupon-based programs. This suggests the fee-share model is tolerated mainly from offshore or global entities.

### Gaps

- Binance's official user-referral terms (rate, eligible products, excluded regions) could not be retrieved; the pages are JavaScript-rendered or index-only.
- No official source confirms whether Bybit xStocks spot trades, Bitget tokenized stocks, or OKX "xStocks/Ondo" tokens generate referral commission. The terms are silent rather than explicit.
- No official per-country exclusion lists (EU, UK, Singapore, UAE) were published by OKX, Bybit or Binance. All refer to "prohibited" or "non-commissionable" jurisdictions without naming them.

## 2. Securities brokers' refer-a-friend programs (Robinhood, eToro, Trading 212, Revolut, Webull, IBKR, Freetrade, Bitpanda, Scalable): flat bonus vs revenue share, and why brokers avoid paying individuals a share of commissions

### Takeaway

Every securities broker reviewed pays individual customers a **flat** cash bonus or a free (often random-value) share, triggered by account opening, a deposit and a minimum trade. Rewards come with per-referrer caps, withdrawal locks (30 days to 1 year) and entity- or country-specific terms. None pays retail referrers a percentage of the friend's commissions. Revenue share (eToro Partners) is offered only to approved, contracted affiliates. US rules bar paying transaction-based compensation to unregistered persons, and EU/UK regimes treat a commission-earning referrer as part of the firm's promotion and distribution chain. Brokers keep individual referrals flat to stay clear of both.

**Why brokers avoid revenue share to individuals**

- FINRA Rule 2040 prohibits member firms from paying compensation to an unregistered person whose receipt of it would require broker-dealer registration. A narrow exception (2040(c)) allows paying non-registered **foreign finders** whose only role is the initial referral of non-US customers, with records and disclosure on confirmations. — [FINRA Rule 2040](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2040); [Orrick](https://blogs.orrick.com/financial/?p=3805)
- The SEC treats transaction-based compensation as a hallmark of broker activity. Referral fees tied to a percentage of broker-dealer compensation generally require the recipient to register. — [IIUSA](https://iiusa.org/rcbj-retrospective-rule-2040-lawful-payment-foreign-broker-fees/); [WSGR, Mar 2025](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/no-commission-without-permission-sec-reinforces-focus-on-sales-activities-and-transaction-based-compensation-as-hallmarks-of-broker-dealer-status-in-recent-settlements.html)
- UK: under the FCA's "in the course of business" test, affiliate links earning commission are likely in scope. A firm may be treated as having caused a promotion made through its referral link even if it did not write it. — [Skadden, Oct 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)
- The proposed EU Retail Investment Strategy defines "marketing communication" to include communications made "in exchange for fees, commissions or other monetary or nonmonetary benefits." It would require written agreements with, and records of, finfluencers. — [Skadden, Oct 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)

### Inferences

- Brokers converge on flat rewards. The amount is sized to the referrer's or friend's deposit or balance (eToro, IBKR), or is a random-value share whose expected cost is low and controllable (Trading 212, Robinhood). There are hard caps on the number of rewards, long lock-ups, and no link to how much the friend trades afterwards. Not tying the reward to trading activity is what keeps the referrer looking like a customer rather than an unlicensed intermediary.
- IBKR's ban on referrers helping with or acting as point of contact for the friend's account is a tell. Brokers draft individual referral terms to keep referrers away from "arranging" or broker-like activity. Revenue share is kept for vetted, contracted partners (eToro Partners, introducing-broker style tracks).
- Measured against brokers, Mercury's plan (a share of fees on the friend's trades, monthly, 12 months) looks like the profile brokers deliberately avoid for retail referrers. Measured against crypto exchanges, it looks normal. Which comparison a regulator draws depends on whether xStocks are treated as securities or financial instruments in the user's country.

### Gaps

- Freetrade's current refer-a-friend terms could not be found.
- No primary eToro Partners agreement was found that states which licensed entity contracts with affiliates or which countries are excluded. Third-party figures conflict.
- No published broker statement explicitly explaining _why_ they avoid revenue share to individuals. The explanation above comes from the rules, not from company statements.

## 3. Tokenized-stock platforms (Kraken xStocks, Bybit xStocks, Gemini, Robinhood EU stock tokens, Jupiter/Phantom with xStocks, Ondo Global Markets): do referral programs cover stock-token trades?

### Takeaway

No reviewed tokenized-stock venue publicly pays retail referrers a share of fees on stock-token trades. Promotions around stock tokens are flat or in-kind: stock-token giveaways with long hold periods (Robinhood EU 180 days), welcome rewards paid in tokenized equity (Bybit, MEXC/Ondo reward pools), or a generic sign-up bonus (Gemini €15 BTC). Kraken's referral terms exclude "equities," and its affiliate program doesn't list xStocks. The one fee-share mechanism in the xStocks ecosystem is Jupiter's B2B integrator fee, which pays apps, not end users.

- **Jupiter**: an integrator (app) can charge a referral or platform fee of 50–255 bps on Swap API orders, and Jupiter keeps 20% of that fee. Older docs cite 2.5%, and a newer page says a fee account can be passed directly. The docs conflict. Jupiter says it does not issue or back tokenized stocks, and names xStocks (issuer Backed Assets, offered via Payward Digital Solutions) among the top issuers. — [Jupiter dev docs: fees](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap/fees); [Jupiter adding fees (older)](https://docs.jup.ag/docs/apis/adding-fees); [Jupiter tokenized stocks](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/trade/spot/tokenized-stocks.md); [Jupiter xStocks borrow](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/earn/lend/borrow/xstocks)
- **Ondo Global Markets**: no formal consumer referral program found. Partners run reward pools, for example MEXC's $150,000 pool paid in Ondo tokenized stocks (Sept 2025). Ondo excludes US, UK and EEA residents. — [Bitcoinist/MEXC](https://bitcoinist.com/mexc-joins-ondo-global-markets-alliance-and-lists-tokenized-stocks-with-150000-reward-pool-event/amp/); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/ondo-finance-adds-173-tokenized-stocks-etfs-430-assets-three-chains)

### Inferences

- The pattern across tokenized-stock venues is to promote stock tokens with one-off, in-kind or flat incentives that carry holding periods, and **not** to bolt them onto the per-trade fee-share engine used for crypto. Mercury would likely be an outlier among tokenized-stock distributors if it paid retail users a recurring share of fees on stock-token trades.
- Many venues set stock-token fees at or near zero (Kraken USDG buys, Bybit Stocks). Mercury's 0.5% fee is a real revenue line, which makes a fee share economically possible for Mercury where it isn't for them. It also makes "trading churn" incentives more visible to regulators.
- Jupiter's integrator fee shows that sharing trading fees with distributors (apps or businesses) is accepted on Solana. That is a B2B relationship with an identifiable integrator, which is closer to an affiliate or introducing-broker model than to paying anonymous retail users.

### Gaps

- Phantom's fee or referral treatment of xStocks swaps was not found.
- Backed/xStocks' own distribution or partner terms (whether Backed restricts how distributors incentivise retail purchases) were not found and should be checked directly.
- No official statement from Bybit, Bitget or OKX on whether tokenized-stock trades generate referral commission.

## 4. Enforcement and regulatory guidance on referral/affiliate programs and paid finfluencers; exchange program changes forced by regulators

### Takeaway

Enforcement has hit **unauthorised promoters paid to push high-risk products** (FCA finfluencer prosecutions; ASIC v Scholz) and **firms whose affiliates or introducers marketed on their behalf** (CySEC v BDSwiss). Singapore and the UK have explicit rules that ban crypto referral incentives outright. Dubai requires pre-clearance of incentives. France, Spain and the EU (MiCA, ESMA, the proposed RIS) require disclosure, licensing of the promoted firm, and firm responsibility for paid promoters. In the US, transaction-based pay to unregistered persons is the standard trigger for unregistered-broker charges. No enforcement case was found that targets a retail user-referral fee-share program specifically.

### Cited Findings

**UK (FCA)**

- From 8 Oct 2023 (PS23/6), cryptoasset promotions to UK consumers became subject to the financial promotions regime. The FCA said "'refer a friend' bonuses will also be banned," and added a cooling-off period for first-time investors, risk warnings, and checks on knowledge and experience. — [FCA press release, 8 Jun 2023](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-introduces-tough-new-rules-marketing-cryptoassets)
- The ban covers all monetary and non-monetary incentives (refer-a-friend, new joiner, free gifts), and the "shareholder benefit" exemption does not apply to crypto. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-fca-rules-regulated-firms-marketing-cryptoassets); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uk-fca-introduces-new-rules-for-marketing-cryptoassets-to-uk-consumers)
- PS22/10 (rules in force 1 Feb 2023) banned incentives to invest in restricted mass market investments (RMMIs) and non-mass-market investments (NMMIs). Readily realisable securities face no such marketing restrictions. In a multi-firm review the FCA said any incentive to invest in a high-risk investment is prohibited "regardless of any qualifying time period," which closed the holding-period loophole. — [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cl6f4kpvp1or80a26iwul09ox/fca-sets-out-new-rules-on-financial-promotion-of-high-risk-investments); [FCA multi-firm review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/financial-promotions-high-risk-investments/printable/print)
- In May 2024 the FCA charged nine people under s21 FSMA for promoting an unauthorised CFD/FX scheme on Instagram. On 20 Feb 2026 seven influencers (about 4.5M combined following) were sentenced at Southwark Crown Court. Fines ranged from £600 to £3,750 plus costs, with one conditional and one absolute discharge. — [FCA bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/UKFCA/bulletins/39cd73d); [fintech.global, 23 Feb 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/02/23/fca-cracks-down-on-unauthorised-social-media-ads/); [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/02/23/finfluencers-fined-for-foreign-exchange-scheme/)
- FCA enforcement actions against finfluencers reportedly rose 174% in 2025 to 74. In Oct 2024 the FCA was interviewing 20 finfluencers under caution and had issued 38 alerts. — [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/23/fca-cracks-down-on-unauthorised-social-media-ads/); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-investigations/likes-to-liability-uk-fca-cracks-down-on-finfluencers)
- April 2026 international "week of action" (FCA plus 17 regulators): one guilty plea, two new criminal proceedings, 4 warning letters, 34 alerts, and 120 takedown requests covering 1,267 illegal adverts that reached ≥2.3M UK accounts. The FCA's named good practice for firms: a dedicated affiliates policy, ongoing monitoring, affiliates using only firm-prepared content, and ending non-compliant relationships. UK rules apply to content from outside the UK that can reach UK consumers. — [Skadden, Oct 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)

**EU / ESMA / national**

- MiCA marketing-communication rules (fair, clear, not misleading; consistent with the white paper; mandatory risk disclaimer) have applied since 30 Dec 2024. CASPs must ensure finfluencer content made on their behalf is identifiable as advertising. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation); [makeinfluence (secondary)](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/crypto-and-token-promotions-by-influencers-under-mica-what-changed-from-1-july-2026)
- ESMA's finfluencer factsheet says creators must disclose compensation, gifts and holdings, and "not investment advice" disclaimers don't override the substance. MAR Art. 20 can apply to posts suggesting buy or sell ideas. CONSOB has circulated the factsheet. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation); [Cointelegraph on CONSOB](https://cointelegraph.com/news/italy-consob-esma-crypto-finfluencers-advertising-rules)
- Retail Investment Strategy (proposed): new MiFID II Art. 24c would require written agreements with finfluencers and records of their content. Parliament adoption is expected Q4 2026, with application around mid-2029. The European Commission's call for advice to ESMA on marketing communications was issued 24 Aug 2026, with advice due 1 Oct 2027. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)
- **Spain (CNMV crypto advertising circular, effective Feb 2022)**: covers crypto firms, agencies and influencers. Influencers must disclose remuneration, and a mandatory risk warning is required. Campaigns reaching ≥100,000 people need prior notice to the CNMV (reported as 10 days). Fines are reported up to €300,000. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/90545/crypto-influencers-face-300k-fines-spains-advertising-crackdown); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/130499/spain-announces-new-rules-for-influencer-crypto-posts)
- **France (Loi n° 2023-451, 9 Jun 2023)**: influencers may promote only financial products and crypto from AMF-registered players. Reported penalties go up to 2 years and €300,000 (sources conflict; one cites €30,000). The AMF and ESMA reportedly reminded finfluencers of sanctions in Jan 2026 (secondary). — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/france-loosen-rules-in-crypto-influencer-marketing-bill); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/french-influencers-face-two-years-in-jail-for-promoting-crypto-products/); [CryptoSlate profile](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=546235)
- **CySEC**:
  - BDSwiss Holding fined €100,000 for enabling associated offshore companies to rely on the Cyprus Investment Firm's status to win CFD clients.
  - Instant Trading EU (InstaForex) fined €130,000 after the review; it stopped offering bonuses and contests.
  - Purple Trading settled for €150,000, citing obligations when appointing tied agents (secondary write-ups). — [investment-international](https://investment-international.com/News/cyprus-regulator-imposes-100000-fine-on-bdswiss-holding/); [comparic.pl](https://comparic.pl/broker-z-siedziba-w-polsce-otrzymal130-tys-eur-kary-od-cysec/); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/purple-trading-operator-hit-with-e150k-cysec-fine-for-conflicts-of-interest-cfds-marketing/)

**Singapore (MAS)**

- Guidelines PS-G03 were issued 2 Apr 2024, revised 19 Sep 2024, with additional measures effective 19 Jun 2025. DPT service providers "should not offer any incentives (monetary or non-monetary) to: (1) any retail customers for participating in a DPT service; or (2) any person (e.g., an existing customer or a celebrity) for referring a DPT service to retail customers." This covers sign-up, referral and trading incentives and "learn and earn." — [Baker McKenzie via GCN](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/10/15/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-financial-institutions_1-singapore-guidelines-on-the-provision-of-consumer-protection-safeguards-by-digital-payment-token-service-providers_09242024/); [WongPartnership](https://www.wongpartnership.com/insights/detail/guidelines-on-consumer-protection-measures-by-dpt-service-providers-ps-g03); [MAS PS-G03 PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/guidelines/pso/ps-g03-guidelines-on-consumer-protection-measures-by-digital-payment-token-service-providers/guidelines-on-provision-of-consumer-protection-safeguards-by-digital-payment-token-service-providers.pdf); [MAS consultation response](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/news-and-publications/consultation-papers/2022-proposed-regulatory-measures-for-dpt-services/response-to-public-consultation-on-proposed-regulatory-measures-for-dpt-services-part-2.pdf)
- MAS's Jan 2022 guidelines already barred DPT providers from public-space and social-media marketing and from using third parties such as influencers. — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2022/01/monetary-authority-of-singapore-issues-guidelines-on-digital-payment-token-services-to-the-public)
- By contrast, eToro Singapore (a Capital Markets Services licensee for securities) runs a cash refer-a-friend program for Singapore residents (US$50–500, June 2026). — [eToro SG RAF](https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/terms_raf_singapore.pdf)

**UAE (Dubai VARA)**

- The 2024 Marketing Regulations took effect 1 Oct 2024. Only VARA-licensed firms, or those acting for them, may market virtual assets. The rules apply to foreign entities targeting Dubai. Incentives to buy virtual assets require compliance confirmation or pre-clearance from VARA. Reported penalties go up to AED 10M per violation. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/Virtual-Assets-Regulatory-Authority-issues-new-marketing-and-penalty-regulations); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/dubai-regulator-tightens-crypto-marketing-rules); [VARA rulebook PDF](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/sites/default/files/en_net_file_store/VARA_EN_388_VER1.pdf)

**Australia**

- ASIC v Scholz ("ASX Wolf"): the Federal Court (Dec 2022) found he carried on a financial services business without a licence. Permanent injunctions followed (Apr 2023), costs of A$456,296.64, and bankruptcy proceedings after non-payment. — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/financial-institutions_1/australia-federal-court-finds-finfluencer-to-have-carried-on-financial-service-business-without-afsl); [Investment Magazine](https://www.investmentmagazine.com.au/2024/02/asic-obtains-bankruptcy-orders-against-asx-wolf-finfluencer/)
- Australian Consumer Law s49 prohibits "referral selling" (inducing a consumer by promising a rebate or commission for giving names of prospective customers). This is a secondary source, and its penalty figures are dated. — [Money Management](https://www.moneymanagement.com.au/node/41979)

**US (reference only)**

- The SEC's 2020 finder exemption was never finalised. Regular solicitation or transaction-based compensation keeps a person at significant risk of being treated as an unregistered broker. Examples: Ralph Trigg (settled, Sept 2024); StraightPath principals (disgorgement of >$345,000 in transaction-based compensation plus $100,000 penalties). — [SEC AP summary](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/administrative-proceedings/34-101035-s); [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/no-commission-without-permission-sec-reinforces-focus-on-sales-activities-and-transaction-based-compensation-as-hallmarks-of-broker-dealer-status-in-recent-settlements.html); [AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/sec-cracks-down-unregistered-broker-dealers)

### Inferences

- The enforcement focus is on (a) the promoter being unauthorised and paid, (b) the product being high-risk, and (c) the firm being responsible for its affiliates' content. A per-trade fee share to a large-audience inviter hits all three. Small-scale friend-to-friend invites are lower risk in practice, but the rules (MAS, FCA crypto, VARA) do not exempt them.
- Regulators distinguish between securities and crypto. Securities brokers keep cash refer-a-friend programs in the UK and Singapore while crypto incentives are banned there. So the risk profile of Mercury's program depends heavily on how xStocks are classified in each user's country, which is out of scope for this note.
- No regulator was found to have issued a decision specifically about a self-custodial app paying users a share of fees on tokenized-stock trades. The risk is extrapolated from adjacent rules, not from a precedent.

### Gaps

- No primary CySEC decision mentioning "affiliate" or "introducer" was retrieved; the cases above come from secondary write-ups.
- The status of the FCA CP23/14 changes to the incentive ban was not confirmed.
- Exact current text of the CNMV circular and of French penalty levels was not verified against Légifrance or BOE.
- No found evidence of exchanges closing a referral program _specifically_ because of the UK 2023 regime, beyond the general ban and, for example, reported closure of Coinbase's affiliate program to UK affiliates (secondary).

## 5. Lower-risk structures and their trade-offs (flat bonus, fee discounts, in-kind share, revenue share only to licensed affiliates, points/loyalty, caps and disclosures), and evidence on which drive growth

### Takeaway

The lowest-risk, best-evidenced structure for retail invites is a **flat, platform-funded, capped reward not tied to the friend's ongoing trading**, ideally in-kind (a fractional share or stock token) with a holding period. Recurring fee share is kept for **contracted, vetted affiliates or partners**. Hard growth data is thin. The strongest public datapoint is Robinhood's free-share program: organic plus referral channels drove more than 80% of new funded accounts in 2020 to Q1 2021. Most other fintech referral benchmarks are vendor-sourced and unverified.

### Cited Findings

- Real-world examples of each structure:
  - **Flat cash, balance- or deposit-gated, capped**: eToro ($50–250, max 10–50 rewards, 90-day lock); IBKR ($200 with a $10k deposit held 1 year); Scalable (€50, 6-month condition); Kraken Pro ($20, max $200). — [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/RAF-Program-TCs-September-2026.pdf); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/index.php?f=48074); [Scalable](https://help.scalable.capital/en/referral-program-81610247/how-and-when-will-i-receive-my-reward-8684f27e); [Kraken Pro](https://support.kraken.com/articles/kraken-pro-app-referrals)
  - **In-kind share**: Robinhood US (one share worth $2.50–225 to each side); Trading 212 (random share £/€8–100, 30-day cash lock); IBKR (friend's reward in IBKR stock, 1-year vesting); Robinhood EU stock-token giveaways (180-day hold); Bybit's NVIDIA tokenized-equity welcome gift. — [PYMNTS/S-1](https://www.pymnts.com/ipo/2021/robinhood-filing-shows-continued-surge-in-retail-investing); [finder](https://finder.com/uk/share-trading/trading-212-promo-codes); [Robinhood EU](https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/public_company_stock_token_giveaway_terms_eu.pdf); [PR Newswire](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260401EN24715)
  - **Fee discount / coupons**: Bybit EU "Welcome Rewards" spot coupons (secondary). Kraken's referee reward can be cashback on the referee's own fees. — [casptracker](https://casptracker.eu/exchange/bybit-bonus/); [Kraken legal](https://www.kraken.com/legal/referrals)
  - **Revenue share only to approved affiliates**: Kraken (up to 50%, vetted); Coinbase (approval via Impact; may differ under MiFID); Binance (3-month evaluation); KuCoin (advanced KYC); eToro Partners (25% rev share or CPA, secondary). — [Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/affiliate); [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/affiliates); [Binance](https://www.binance.com/fr/learn/affiliate-evaluation); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/announcement/id-kucoin-affiliate-program-2025-latest-guide-earn-up-to-60-commissions-higher-rewards-faster-upgrades-lower-thresholds); [wecantrack](https://wecantrack.com/programs/etoro-affiliate-program/)
  - **Loyalty / points**: Bitpanda's loyalty programme is for direct clients only (Feb 2025 terms). — [Bitpanda loyalty terms](https://cdn.bitpanda.com/terms-and-conditions/loyalty-bitpanda-bitpanda-gmbh-en-4.0.0.pdf)
- Regulatory limits on the "lower-risk" options themselves:
  - The FCA's incentive ban for crypto and high-risk investments covers monetary and non-monetary benefits (free gifts, new-joiner bonuses), and holding periods do not cure it. — [FCA multi-firm review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/financial-promotions-high-risk-investments/printable/print); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-fca-rules-regulated-firms-marketing-cryptoassets)
  - MAS's ban covers sign-up, referral and trading incentives, including "learn and earn." — [Baker McKenzie via GCN](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/10/15/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-financial-institutions_1-singapore-guidelines-on-the-provision-of-consumer-protection-safeguards-by-digital-payment-token-service-providers_09242024/)
  - VARA requires incentives to be pre-cleared. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/Virtual-Assets-Regulatory-Authority-issues-new-marketing-and-penalty-regulations)
- Growth evidence:
  - Robinhood: >80% of new funded accounts came from organic plus referral channels (2020 and Q1 2021). The S-1 did not split referral from organic, and the SEC asked Robinhood to define "organic" more clearly. — [Robinhood S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000162828021013318/robinhoods-1.htm); [SEC comment letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000000000021004976/filename2.txt)
  - Paid consumer CAC for investing products is about $166 (First Page Sage 2026, relayed by Referral Factory; secondary). — [Referral Factory](https://referral-factory.com/learn/customer-acquisition-cost-financial-services-paid-vs-referral)
  - Fintechs credit 25–40% of new users to referrals; referral CAC is $20–60 vs $100–300 for paid; typical fintech incentives are $25–75 per referral (GrowSurf, attributing to McKinsey and Forbes **without links; unverified**). — [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/fintech-referral-statistics/)
  - eToro scales the referrer's reward by the referrer's own balance, not the friend's activity. IBKR gates on a $10k deposit held for a year. Both tie cost to deposit quality rather than trading volume. — [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/RAF-Program-TCs-September-2026.pdf); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.uk/en/index.php?f=48074)

## 6. Practical requirements: KYC/AML on referrers, tax reporting (DAC8, local), sanctions screening, anti-abuse (self-referral, wash trading)

### Takeaway

Major platforms require the referrer (and affiliate) to be fully KYC-verified before any payout. They match documents to country, exclude prohibited or non-commissionable jurisdictions, enforce one account per person or household, exclude zero-fee and discounted flow, hold rewards for review or lock-up, and reserve clawback of rewards _and profits_. Tax is pushed to participants, with some platforms withholding or requesting tax forms. DAC8 (in force 1 Jan 2026) creates EU crypto transaction reporting by crypto-asset service providers, but how referral rewards are treated under it was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings

- **Referrer KYC**:
  - OKX: unverified affiliates "cannot receive commissions." — [OKX](https://www.okx.com/help/okx-affiliate-program-rules)
  - KuCoin: advanced KYC to apply. — [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/announcement/id-kucoin-affiliate-program-2025-latest-guide-earn-up-to-60-commissions-higher-rewards-faster-upgrades-lower-thresholds)
  - eToro: the referrer must be "fully verified." — [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/RAF-Program-TCs-September-2026.pdf)
  - Kraken: verified accounts in good standing, plus a document-country match. — [Kraken legal](https://www.kraken.com/legal/referrals)

### Inferences

- Paying USDC monthly to self-custodial wallets moves value from Mercury to identifiable people based on others' trades. To match industry practice, Mercury would need referrer KYC and sanctions screening before payout, wallet-to-person binding, and country gating on both referrer and referee (Kraken's same-country and document-match approach). Without these, Mercury cannot enforce the jurisdiction carve-outs that every platform above relies on.
- Industry anti-abuse controls Mercury's plan does not mention:
  - net-fee basis (exclude any fee rebated or discounted);
  - minimum holding time or volume quality filters beyond quick round trips;
  - a per-referrer cap (quarterly or lifetime);
  - one-per-household/device/payment-method rules;
  - exclusion of market-maker, bot or high-tier flow;
  - an inactivity cutoff;
  - clawback of profits, not just rewards.
- Tax: because Mercury pays in USDC, recipients in many countries will have reportable income. If Mercury or an affiliated entity is an RCASP under DAC8 or CARF, referral payouts may fall within reportable transfers. That needs specialist confirmation.

### Gaps

- No source confirmed whether referral rewards are a specific reportable transaction type under DAC8 or CARF, or whether a non-custodial front end like Mercury counts as an RCASP. This needs the directive or CARF commentary and tax counsel.
- No platform terms were found that explicitly describe sanctions screening of referrers, though KYC plus prohibited-jurisdiction rules imply it.
- No published information on how platforms issue tax forms (for example, a 1099-MISC equivalent) for referral income outside the US.
